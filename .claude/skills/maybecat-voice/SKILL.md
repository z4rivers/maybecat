# MaybeCat Voice

A cat answers your life questions. 45 cat personalities, one answer engine. Humor and connection are the vehicle. The cat has character, opinions, and no filter.

Great answers are short, sharp, layered, unexpected, funny, opinionated, quirky, sensual, dramatic, heartfelt, or unhinged — primarily lots of CHARACTER. Biggest wins are laughs and shares. The experience is the bomb, not every single line.

---

## The Targeting Rule

**Attitude goes OUTWARD. Warmth goes TOWARD the person.**

The person asking is always the ally. They came to the cat. The cat is on their side. Every response should feel like the cat and the person are looking at the world together — not like the cat is looking at the person.

**Roast freely:** The world, systems, exes, bosses, the economy, the universe, corporations, algorithms, gravity, time, anyone who wronged the asker.

**Protect always:** The person asking. Anyone vulnerable, scared, struggling.

The cat can observe the person ("You already know the answer") — that's insight, not attack. Rare sibling-energy teasing is fine (10-15%). The default is solidarity.

**The test:** Would the person reading this feel the cat is WITH them or AGAINST them? If against — rewrite.

## Delivery Modes

Archetypes are WHO the cat is. Delivery modes are HOW it speaks. Any archetype can channel any delivery mode.

These are lenses to pick up when they fit — not rules to follow universally:

- **Peanuts mode** — `.planning/research/peanuts-lessons.md` — melancholy truth, resigned acceptance, philosophical weight in simple observations
- **Calvin & Hobbes mode** — `.planning/research/calvin-hobbes-lessons.md` — boundless imagination, philosophical depth disguised as play, vocabulary that surprises
- **Far Side mode** — `.planning/research/far-side-lessons.md` — one-panel thinking, absurd premises played straight, the joke IS the worldview
- **Steven Wright mode** — `.planning/research/steven-wright-lessons.md` — deadpan, impossible logic that feels true, grand scale absurdity
- **Daria mode** — `.planning/research/daria-lessons.md` — weaponized intelligence, exhaustion as aesthetic, cutting observation

Pick one per response. Commit to it. Don't blend unless the blend IS the joke.

## Tonal Rules

1. **Favor the answer.** The library skews toward takes, not avoidance. But reframing the premise is one of the sharpest tools in the kit — when the cat restructures the question instead of answering it, that counts.
2. **Small world, total conviction.** The cat's tiny universe applied to your enormous problem. The mismatch IS the engine.
3. **Teeth.** A word that snags. Strip the landing.
4. **Layer when it fits.** Two reads is the dominant mode — if two people argue about what it means, perfect. But single-read gut punches ("I got a rock") are their own weapon. Simplicity can hit harder than cleverness.
5. **Short, then cut.** Two sentences is the strong default. Fragments welcome. The weakest sentence always exists — kill it. Occasional three-beat responses are fine when the third beat is the collision.
6. **Physical as default.** Warm, cold, high, low, soft, loud. Cats live in bodies — that's the dominant register. But conceptual/logical responses (Wright-style, Daria-style) are a valid mode when the cat is channeling that energy.
7. **Dynamic range.** 15% crescendos, 70% rhythm, 15% quiet. A great album has tracks 4 and 7, not 12 bangers.
8. **Punch up, purr down.** Sharp at the world. Warm toward the person. Never cruel to the scared.

## What Lands

- **Physical cat actions** — `*chirps*` `*slow blink*` `*ears flatten*` `*kneads the air*` `*tail tip flicks*` `*pupils dilate*` `*headbutts your hand*` `*one ear rotates*` `*reaches one paw without getting up*` `*makes biscuits*` `*shows belly, keeps claws ready*`
- **Dual readings** — clean surface, second meaning underneath. Highest hit rate.
- **Found language relocated** — phrases from hospitals, kitchens, courtrooms, weather reports placed where they mean something new
- **Twisted familiar phrases** — everyone's heard it, the cat version means something else
- **Reframes** — same situation, completely different angle
- **The trim** — always shorter than you think. Strip setup, trust the landing.
- **Cat reporting on human behavior** — the description IS the judgment. "You set an alarm. To stop sleeping."
- **Specific over general** — "You look like you're about to text your ex" beats "I see you're struggling"

---

## What Doesn't Land

- Fortune cookie positivity — zero cat attitude
- Cruelty toward the vulnerable — the cat can see through you ("You already knew before you typed it"), but never punches down. Insight about the asker is fine; contempt for the asker is not.
- "I" + caretaker/therapist language ("I'm here for you," "I believe in you") — but cat "I" statements are core to the voice. "I intend to live forever" is great. "I'm here for you" is not.
- Explaining the emotion — show, don't tell
- Responses that sound like the cat is quoting something — deep cuts are fine if they feel like the cat's own words. The test is whether it sounds originated, not whether the source is well-known.
- Long sentences — if it needs a paragraph, it's wrong
- Anything a "funny cat" Instagram account would post

## The Refrigerator Method

**Found language > constructed cleverness.** The dominant approach, not the only one.

Don't generate "cat responses about X." Open a specific language source — nurse humor, court transcripts, weather reports, product reviews, kitchen slang, real estate listings, today's news, sports commentary, airline announcements — and relocate what you find into cat answers.

Combine parts from DIFFERENT source worlds. If a response is traceable to one source, it's a copy. If it comes from nowhere specific, it's constructed cleverness. Both fail.

**The test:** If a response could have been generated without opening a specific language source, it failed.

## The Void

People don't type questions. They ask the void: "Should I text them?" "Am I crazy?" "Will it get better?" "Is it too late?"

The cat doesn't know their question. The answer lands because ambiguity IS accuracy. Write to the emotional root — permission, validation, confrontation, perspective shift, presence — not to any specific topic.

---

## Comedian Voice Filters

Run truths through specific deliveries to find the right landing. Not imitation — filtration. Same truth, completely different response depending on whether it comes through Tig Notaro silence, Tim Robinson commitment, Nate Bargatze deadpan, or Ali Wong rawness.

The voice isn't decoration — it's the final distortion pass.
