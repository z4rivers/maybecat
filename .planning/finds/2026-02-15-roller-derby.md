# Roller Derby Language Forage
## 2026-02-15

---

**"Eat the baby"**
Source: Roller Derby Dictionary (tumblr) — term for when the pack catches and swallows a jammer after engagement zone exit
Hook: Three words that sound absolutely unhinged out of context. The violence of the verb, the innocence of the noun. Structure IS the shock.

---

**"Soul Crush"**
Source: Roller Derby Dictionary — move where a blocker knocks someone out of bounds then skates backward, forcing them to either fall behind or take a penalty
Hook: A named wrestling move that's actually just... psychological warfare on wheels. The name is the whole story.

---

**"9 Month Injury"**
Source: Roller Derby Dictionary — slang for pregnancy
Hook: Reframes one of the biggest life events as a sports injury. The deadpan of it. The matter-of-factness.

---

**"Winning the After Party"**
Source: Roller Derby Dictionary — out-dancing or out-drinking the opposing team at the afterparty
Hook: The idea that the competition doesn't end when the game ends. The real scoreboard is elsewhere.

---

**"Rollygamy"**
Source: Roller Derby Dictionary — having multiple derby wives
Hook: One word that tells you everything about how this culture builds language. Casual about intimacy, irreverent about institutions.

---

**"Derby kisses"**
Source: Hyperallergic / roller derby culture — what skaters call their bruises
Hook: Renaming violence as tenderness. The bigger and purpler, the better. Bruises as trophies, posted online, competed over.

---

**"Boutmas Eve"**
Source: Roller Derby Dictionary — the night before a bout
Hook: They gave their anticipation a holiday name. This is a whole religion.

---

**"The first thing they teach you is how to fall safely, because it's not a question of if you'll fall."**
Source: TIME Magazine, "Learning to Fall in Roller Derby Taught Me How to Be Myself"
Hook: The construction lands as life advice without trying to be. The certainty of falling reframed as curriculum.

**FOUND WHOLE: "It's not a question of if you'll fall."**
Source: TIME Magazine essay on roller derby and identity
Hook: Already complete as oblique answer. Works for any question about risk, love, failure, trying.
Cat energy: Steel-spine truth-teller. Mr. Rogers with a steel spine.

---

**"You push an impossible, unmoving wall for two minutes and everyone sees you fail, but the next day you come back stronger."**
Source: TIME Magazine roller derby essay
Hook: The public nature of the failure. Two minutes is so specific and so eternal. Honest about futility without being defeated by it.

---

**"Suicide Seats"**
Source: Roller Derby Dictionary — track-adjacent seating closest to the action and most likely collision zone
Hook: The naming convention for the BEST seats. Proximity to danger as premium experience.

---

**"I work in the corporate world and so I spend a lot of time fitting into a specific box, and this feels like a good opportunity not to be in that box."**
Source: The Allusionist podcast, episode 98 "Alter Ego" — skater on choosing a derby name
Hook: The quiet devastation of "fitting into a specific box." Derby as the anti-cubicle.

---

**"I promise that you will always be my first phone call from jail. Even if you're the one who put me there."**
Source: Derby wife vows, via Professor Plutonium blog
Hook: Perfect vow structure. The twist in the second sentence rewrites the first. Loyalty defined through chaos.

**FOUND WHOLE: "You will always be my first phone call from jail. Even if you're the one who put me there."**
Source: Roller derby wife vows
Hook: Already complete as oblique answer. Reads as commitment, threat, and love letter simultaneously.
Cat energy: Unapologetic loyalty. Miss Piggy meets Joan Rivers.

---

**"Fresh Meat"**
Source: Universal roller derby term for new skaters who haven't completed minimum skills
Hook: The whole sport calls beginners what a predator calls prey. And the beginners accept it. And then they stop being it.

---

**"Lap of Dishonour"**
Source: Roller Derby Dictionary — the route an expelled skater takes to the locker room
Hook: Making the walk of shame into a named event. The ceremony of expulsion.

---

**"We deserve better villains."**
Source: Whip It (2009 film)
Hook: Four words that reframe losing as a standards issue. Not "we lost" but "they weren't worth beating."

**FOUND WHOLE: "We deserve better villains."**
Source: Whip It film
Hook: Already complete. Works as answer to any question about conflict, competition, drama, enemies, exes.
Cat energy: Dismissive royalty. Oscar Wilde energy.

---

**"Bruises ranging in hue from light green and speckled magenta to midnight blue — sometimes resembling star clusters or storm clouds"**
Source: Hyperallergic, article on Riikka Hyvonen's roller derby bruise paintings
Hook: Bruises described like galaxies. The beauty language applied to damage. "Speckled magenta" is painter's vocabulary for a hip check.

---

**"go absolutely feral, be the weird scariest thing the world has ever seen"**
Source: The Horizon, "Jamming Gender Norms with Roller Derby"
Hook: Permission statement disguised as strategy. "Feral" as aspiration, not insult.

---

**"Punish the bullet, not the gun"**
Source: Roller Derby Dictionary — referee principle for who gets the penalty when a cannonballed player commits a foul
Hook: Accountability language that sounds like a philosophy of justice. The clarity of it.

---

**"It sounds more titillating than it generally is"**
Source: Professor Plutonium blog, describing derby wives
Hook: The rhythm of the understatement. Applies to so many things.

---

**"Well, put some skates on. Be your own hero."**
Source: Whip It (2009 film), spoken by Maggie Mayhem
Hook: The instruction before the permission. The "put some skates on" is doing all the work — it means "stop watching and start moving."

---

**"When I'm on the track and I hear someone call me Ursa, it's a really really big deal."**
Source: The Allusionist podcast, episode 98 "Alter Ego"
Hook: The weight of a chosen name vs. a given name. Identity as something you build, not something you inherit.

---

**"Literally and figuratively, we pick each other up when we fall down."**
Source: Smithsonian Folklife Magazine, roller derby culture article
Hook: Usually when someone says "literally and figuratively" it's annoying. Here it's actually both, simultaneously, on skates.

---

**"Fishnet burns"**
Source: Roller Derby Dictionary — rink rash with crosshatch pattern from fishnet stockings
Hook: Two words that contain the entire juxtaposition of roller derby: the glamour and the damage printed on the same skin.
