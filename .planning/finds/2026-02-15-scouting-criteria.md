# Scouting Criteria / Draft Evaluation Language
## Foraging: 2026-02-15

The language of assessing living beings with clinical authority. NFL/NBA/AFL draft reports, horse racing form, livestock judging, dog show breed standards. The gap between the metric and the soul.

---

**"Looks like Tarzan, plays like Jane."**
Source: NFL scouting glossary (widespread usage)
Hook: The universal gap between what someone looks like they should be and what they actually are.

**"Potential: the quality of not sucking yet."**
Source: ESPN Page 2 NFL Draft Glossary (satirical definitions)
Hook: The most honest definition of potential ever written.

**"Raw talent: a football player who cannot actually play football."**
Source: ESPN Page 2 NFL Draft Glossary
Hook: The paradox of being praised for what you haven't done.

**"Maxed out: a prospect who already can play football, and is therefore undesirable."**
Source: ESPN Page 2 NFL Draft Glossary
Hook: The perverse logic of wanting someone unfinished over someone competent.

**"One-paced: a horse that does not quicken when the speed of the race increases."**
Source: Horse racing commentary terminology
Hook: Devastating when applied to a person. Some people just don't have another gear.

**"Needs some glass in his diet."**
Source: NFL Scouting Glossary (The Ringer)
Hook: Translation: plays soft. But the image -- needing to eat glass to toughen up -- is wild.

**"Cow on ice."**
Source: NFL Scouting Glossary (The Ringer)
Hook: Describes disorganized footwork. Perfect image for anyone flailing through life.

**"Carries himself proudly, with an air of distinction and dignity peculiar to himself."**
Source: AKC Poodle breed standard
Hook: A sentence written about a dog that describes the person you're either dating or avoiding.

**"Stayed on well."**
Source: Horse racing form commentary
Hook: The faint praise of endurance without victory. Finished strong but didn't win.

**"Through the echo of the whistle."**
Source: NFL scouting terminology (Gang Green Nation)
Hook: A player who never stops until the play is truly over. Accidentally beautiful phrase.

**"You can't help the club in the tub."**
Source: NFL scouting saying
Hook: Injured players can't contribute. But also: you can't help anyone from the couch.

**"More ruggedly designed."**
Source: 4-H Livestock Judging Manual (Mississippi State Extension)
Hook: A phrase used to describe cattle that sounds like a Tinder bio for someone who hikes.

**"Moved out freer and easier."**
Source: 4-H Livestock Judging Manual
Hook: Livestock evaluation language for "good movement." Also what happens when you leave a bad relationship.

**"Soft hands: pass catcher with great hands, rarely drops a pass."**
Source: NFL draft scouting terminology
Hook: Used for receivers. Also the most romantic thing you could say about someone.

**"Off the bridle: not travelling well, needs encouragement."**
Source: Horse racing commentary
Hook: Describing a horse that's struggling. Accidentally describes every Monday morning.

**"Has a switch: can flip into game mode."**
Source: NFL intangibles evaluation (The Ringer)
Hook: Scouts looking for the ability to become a different person on demand. Relatable.

**"Trimmer, cleaner patterned."**
Source: 4-H Livestock Judging Manual
Hook: How they describe a well-conditioned animal. Also how you describe an ex who got their life together.

**FOUND WHOLE: "Just a guy."**
Source: NFL scouting term (JAG = "Just A Guy")
Hook: The most devastating three-word assessment. Average. Nothing special. Just a guy.
Cat energy: deadpan devastation -- Mitch Hedberg territory

**FOUND WHOLE: "Doesn't have a lot of tread on the tires."**
Source: NFL draft cliche list
Hook: Fresh, not worn out. But also implies that someone who HAS lived is used goods. Works both ways.
Cat energy: Oscar Wilde inversion -- could be compliment or insult depending on the question

**"Passes the eye test."**
Source: NFL scouting cliche
Hook: Looks right but hasn't proven anything. The entire premise of first impressions.

**"Well-held: clearly beaten by winner."**
Source: Horse racing form guide
Hook: The gentlest possible way to say you lost and it wasn't close.

### Bonus cluster: phrases that accidentally describe relationships

- "High floor, low ceiling" -- reliable but will never surprise you
- "Scheme dependent" -- only works in the right situation
- "Tweener" -- doesn't fit any category, too much of one thing, not enough of another
- "Plug and play" -- ready to go, no development needed
- "Workout warrior" -- impressive in theory, disappointing in practice
- "Glass eater" -- merciless, vicious, unrelenting (offensive linemen, or your ex)
- "Football character" -- toughness, competitiveness, dedication, desire to be great
- "Tight in the hips" -- stiff, struggles turning (or: little chance of success on Dancing With the Stars)
- "Dancing bear" -- huge but surprisingly agile
- "Pulling" -- eager to go faster than allowed
- "Pushed out" -- won easily without effort
- "A locker room guy" -- good to have around even if not the star
- "Kind of a prick on the field" -- desirable trait in offensive linemen
- "Seeing ghosts" -- reacting to pressure that isn't there
