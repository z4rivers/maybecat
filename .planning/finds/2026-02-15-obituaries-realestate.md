## OBITUARY FINDS

**"It pains me to admit it, but apparently, I have passed away."**
Source: Emily DeBrayda Phillips, self-written obituary (2015)
Hook: The "apparently" does all the work -- reluctant acceptance as comedy.

**"So... I was born; I blinked; and it was over."**
Source: Emily DeBrayda Phillips, self-written obituary (2015)
Hook: The compression of an entire life into a semicolon pause.

**"Doug died."**
Source: Douglas Legler, self-written obituary (2015). His daughter said he'd point at long obits and ask, "What, couldn't they hold down a job?"
Hook: Two words. The refusal to perform is the performance.

**"I AM the guy who stole the safe from the Motor View Drive Inn back in June, 1971. I could have left that unsaid, but I wanted to get it off my chest."**
Source: Val "Rocky" Patterson, self-written obituary (2012)
Hook: Deathbed confession as throwaway line. The casual tone is the joke.

**"He respectfully requests six Cleveland Browns pallbearers so the Browns can let him down one last time."**
Source: Scott Entsminger obituary
Hook: Structure does the killing -- "respectfully requests" into a devastating burn.

**"We think he did it on purpose to avoid having to make a decision in the pending presidential election."**
Source: William Ziegler obituary (2016), on dying at 69
Hook: Death as avoidance strategy. The "we think" is chef's kiss.

**"He is survived by his wife, who will now be able to purchase the mink coat which he had always refused her because he believed only minks should wear mink."**
Source: Walter George Bruhl Jr., self-written obituary
Hook: Marriage as long-game negotiation, settled only by death.

**"He was preceded in death by his tonsils and adenoids in 1935, a spinal disc in 1974, a large piece of his thyroid in 1988, and his prostate on March 27, 2000."**
Source: Walter George Bruhl Jr., self-written obituary
Hook: "Preceded in death by" structure hijacked -- body parts leaving him one at a time over decades.

**"Irishman dies from stubbornness, whiskey."**
Source: Chris Connors obituary headline (2016), written by his daughter at his request to "make it funny"
Hook: Cause of death as two-word character study.

**"He grabbed life by the lapels, kissed it, and swung it back onto the dance floor."**
Source: Chris Connors -- the only line he wrote himself in his own obituary
Hook: Life as a dance partner you manhandle. Kinetic, physical, romantic.

**"A plus-sized Jewish lady redneck died in El Paso on Saturday."**
Source: Renay Mandel Corren obituary, written by her son Andy
Hook: Identity as magnificent contradiction. Every adjective fights the next one.

**"She spent it all, folks. She left me nothing but these lousy memories."**
Source: Andy Corren, closing line of his mother Renay's obituary
Hook: "Lousy" doing triple duty -- resentment, love, and the best punchline structure (setup inversion).

**"He drank buttermilk from a martini glass garnished with cornbread."**
Source: Harry Stamps obituary (Mississippi), written by his daughter
Hook: The format clash IS the personality. Highbrow vessel, lowbrow contents.

**"He excelled at outsmarting squirrels and never losing a game of competitive sickness."**
Source: Harry Stamps obituary
Hook: "Competitive sickness" -- the phrase names something everyone recognizes but nobody says.

**"The family asks that in honor of Harry, you write your Congressman and ask for the repeal of Daylight Saving Time. Harry wanted everyone to get back on the Lord's Time."**
Source: Harry Stamps obituary
Hook: Turning an obituary's "in lieu of flowers" into a petty legislative crusade.

**"I died of FOMO due to complications of Bulbar ALS."**
Source: Linda Brossi Murphy, self-written obituary (2025)
Hook: Modern abbreviation crashes into medical reality. Compression creates the shock.

**"After a week-long battle with torso failure and the lasting effects of a 1992 alien abduction."**
Source: Elwood "Buddy" Segeske III obituary
Hook: "Torso failure" is the most honest euphemism for dying ever written. The alien abduction is bonus.

## REAL ESTATE FINDS

**"The roof leaks, the floor creaks, and there's a terrible draft, but this 3 bed, 1.5 bath home is very open concept. And by that we mean the inside is open to the outside because several of the windows are broken."**
Source: Viral Florida listing by Philippa Main, Zephyrhills FL ($69,000)
Hook: "Open concept" redefined. The euphemism detonates from the inside.

**"If you're not interested in crying yourself to sleep every night while you rehab this home, might we suggest tearing it down and building a brand new one in its place? The neighbors would likely thank you."**
Source: Same Florida listing (Philippa Main)
Hook: Agent exhaustion as sales pitch. "The neighbors would thank you" is the knife twist.

**"Have you ever watched HGTV and thought, I could do that!? If so, pack up your tape measure and start Googling how to identify a load-bearing wall because it's time to put your money where your mouth is!"**
Source: Same Florida listing (Philippa Main)
Hook: Calling the buyer's bluff. The "Googling load-bearing wall" is perfect -- it's what they'd actually do.

**"Cozy" = small, if not claustrophobic. "Charming" = old and broken. "Original features" = the appliances are 50 years old. "Needs TLC" = not quite a total tear-down, but plan on spending your life savings.**
Source: Aggregated real estate euphemism translations (multiple sources)
Hook: An entire language built on the gap between what's said and what's meant.

**"Easy freeway access" = you'll need pillows duct-taped to your head to sleep.**
Source: Real estate euphemism decoder (Jeff Shore)
Hook: The image. Pillows duct-taped to your head. That's copywriting.

**"Convenient to public transit" = you'll be able to make eye contact with subway riders as they pass your windows.**
Source: Real estate euphemism translations
Hook: Forced intimacy with strangers, reframed as a feature.

**"Not haunted."**
Source: Actual real estate sign in Fall River, MA (went viral, 25K likes). Originated with New Orleans agent Finis Shelnutt in 2014 who put "haunted" on one side and "not haunted" on the other.
Hook: The denial IS the accusation. Same energy as "I'm fine."

## META-OBSERVATIONS

**The obituary format itself is a compression engine.** When you only get 200 words to summarize a life, everything non-essential gets cut. What remains reveals what actually mattered. The structure ("preceded in death by," "survived by," "in lieu of flowers") becomes a comedic framework when you fill it with unexpected content.

**Real estate euphemisms are an entire dialect of optimistic lying.** The gap between "sun-drenched" and "no curtains will save you" is where comedy lives. Every listing is a negotiation between honesty and hope.

**Both formats share a key trait:** They take a rigid, formal structure and let human personality leak through the cracks. The FORMAT is the straight man. The CONTENT is the comedian.
