# Foraging: Flight Attendant Announcements + IT Support Tickets
Date: 2026-02-15

---

**"If you don't know how to operate one, you probably shouldn't be out in public unsupervised."**
Source: Southwest flight attendant explaining seatbelts (Reader's Digest collection)
Hook: The professional smile barely masking "I can't believe I have to say this" — pure tired authority energy.

**"There may be fifty ways to leave your lover, but there are only four ways out of this airplane."**
Source: Flight attendant safety briefing (multiple airlines, widely attributed)
Hook: Borrowed song lyric reframed as hard constraint. The math of your actual options.

**"We are pleased to have some of the best flight attendants in the industry. Unfortunately, none of them are on this flight."**
Source: Delta Airlines announcement (Wanderings.net collection)
Hook: Self-deprecation as power move. Lowers expectations while owning the room.

**"If you are traveling with more than one small child, decide now which one you love more."**
Source: Southwest flight attendant oxygen mask briefing (multiple sources)
Hook: The cruelest question delivered in the calmest voice. Safety theater meets Sophie's Choice.

**"This is for your comfort and to enhance the appearance of your flight attendants."**
Source: Pilot dimming cabin lights at cruising altitude (widely reported)
Hook: The quiet admission that lighting is doing heavy lifting for all of us.

**"Please stay inside the plane till we land. It's a bit cold outside, and if you walk on the wings it affects the flight pattern."**
Source: Pilot announcement at cruising altitude (Eyeflare collection)
Hook: Stating the obvious as if it's a gentle suggestion, not a survival requirement.

**"This aircraft is equipped to land on water... Once."**
Source: Flight attendant safety briefing (DeMilked collection)
Hook: That devastating one-word addition. The pause before "once" is doing all the work.

**"That was quite a bump, and I know what y'all are thinking. I'm here to tell you it wasn't the airline's fault, it wasn't the pilot's fault, it wasn't the flight attendant's fault... it was the asphalt."**
Source: Southwest flight attendant after hard landing (multiple sources)
Hook: The slow setup, the rhetorical structure of blame-deflection, then the pun lands harder than the plane did.

**"Did we land, or were we shot down?"**
Source: Passenger after hard landing, became crew folklore (FlyerTalk forums)
Hook: Five words. Perfect deadpan. The question you're not supposed to ask.

**"Welcome to Lubbock. Please set your watches to 1962."**
Source: American Eagle pilot (FlyerTalk forums)
Hook: Location as time travel. One sentence demolishes a whole city.

**"Sorry for the delay in taking off, but our automatic baggage smashing equipment is not working, so we are having to smash your bags by hand."**
Source: PSA pilot announcement (FlyerTalk forums)
Hook: Saying the quiet part loud — reframing damage as a service they provide.

**"Application Sunscreen isn't working."**
Source: IT ticket — user meant the window blinds in her office (Ivanti blog)
Hook: The gap between the word someone uses and the word they mean, producing a completely different emergency.

**"We can't send mail more than 500 miles."**
Source: University department chair to IT (Reddit/MakeUseOf "500-mile email" story)
Hook: Absurd constraint stated with total certainty. The user reporting an impossible fact that turned out to be technically true.

**"I-P-C-O-N-F-I-G-E-N-T-E-R"**
Source: IT tech asked user to type "ipconfig" then press Enter. She typed the word "enter." (Ivanti blog)
Hook: Instructions followed with terrifying precision. The letter of the law, not the spirit.

**"I can't get the disc out. I have tried butter knives, screwdrivers, pliers."**
Source: Help desk ticket — user never tried the eject button (PowerAdmin blog)
Hook: Escalating violence against a machine while ignoring the designed solution. Maximum effort, wrong direction.

**"The extension lead had been plugged into itself."**
Source: IT support ticket resolution (Operum Tech blog)
Hook: Self-referential loop as technical diagnosis. Trying to power yourself from yourself.

**"Reattached for your convenience."**
Source: Passive-aggressive corporate email phrase (Coburg Banks translation guide)
Hook: Translation — "I already sent this. You didn't read it. I'm smiling through clenched teeth." Politeness as violence.

**"Per my last email."**
Source: Universal corporate passive-aggression (multiple sources)
Hook: Three words that mean "I already told you this and we both know it." The professional version of "I said what I said."

**"Friendly reminder."**
Source: Corporate email phrase (Coburg Banks translation guide)
Hook: Translation — "It's not friendly. Where's what I asked for?" The word "friendly" doing the opposite of its job.

**"Keyboard Error: Press any key to continue."**
Source: Classic computer error message (Technologizer)
Hook: Asking you to use the thing it just told you doesn't work. The machine equivalent of "pull yourself together."

**"You don't exist. Go away."**
Source: Linux error message after editing passwd file incorrectly (Technologizer comments)
Hook: The computer having an existential crisis about YOU. Blunt rejection from a machine.

**"We should never get here!"**
Source: Cadnetix workstation error message in supposedly unreachable code (Technologizer)
Hook: The developer's panic note, never meant to be seen, now displayed to the user. Behind-the-curtain moment.
