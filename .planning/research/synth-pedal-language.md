### Clipping — What Happens When You Push Past The Limit

- "Clipping occurs when a circuit pushes a signal beyond its maximum capacity" — Wampler Pedals
- "We take the top off, take the bottom off, usually take them both off" — Wampler blog
- "Soft-clipping gradually rounds off the peaks of your signal. This leads to a smoother, warmer, and more natural-sounding distortion" — treaudio.com
- "Hard clipping — the end result doesn't even resemble a sine wave anymore, in fact it looks almost exactly like a square wave" — treaudio.com
- "Soft-clipping feels more dynamic and touch-sensitive. You can clean up the sound by rolling back your guitar's volume knob or playing more lightly" — various sources

### Headroom — Space Before Everything Breaks

- "Headroom is the space a signal can swing within the power rails without hitting the limits" — Anasounds

- "Headroom is clean signal before clipping, distortion is clipping" — Gearspace
- "There will ultimately be a point where increasing the volume going in doesn't increase the volume coming out. Instead, things just get more compressed and distorted, because you've hit the preamp's headroom" — Mission Engineering

## 10. SIGNAL CHAIN, ROUTING & BYPASS

### Signal Chain — Order Matters

- "Just as a great recipe adds ingredients in the correct order, optimizing pedal order lets each effect shine" — Guitar Pedal X
- "The order of effects pedals in your signal chain can have a big impact on the overall sound" — various
- "Drive pedals will radically affect the signal's dynamics and amplify the noise of any pedal that comes before" — various
- "If the noisiest pedal is first in the chain, that noise will be affected and amplified further by every other pedal in the chain" — BOSS Articles
- "The tuner goes before everything because it will hear your guitar best if it gets the cleanest unadulterated signal directly from your guitar" — BOSS Articles

### Wet/Dry/Send & Return

- "A signal path is split in two — one path is left untreated while the other is processed, before the two are blended back together" — Cosmic Loop FX
- "Parallel mixing keeps your dry tone pristine while blending in the processed signals separately" — various
- "Your dry tone never passes through any effect pedal. Pick attack and dynamics stay tight and responsive. You decide exactly how much of each effect to blend in." — parallel mixer description
- "Works much like the Aux Send/Return on a mixing console" — Xotic X-Blender
- "A 3-amp technique where you route the dry signal to a center amp and the wet signals to amps on either side. The guitar's pristine articulation and transients cut clearly through, even if it features dripping-wet reverb" — Sweetwater WDW guide

### True Bypass vs. Buffered Bypass

- "When true bypass, your guitar signal is sent straight through the pedal without being affected in any way" — Perfect Circuit
- "When plugged into a buffered pedal, your guitar signal is being affected even when the pedal is off" — various
- "Signal loss or tone suck happens when a high impedance signal travels through a lot of cable. The more cable it has to travel through, the more the tone is sucked" — Perfect Circuit
- "True bypass means the circuit will not color the tone when bypassed, but doesn't protect against the natural tone loss inherent in a pedalboard" — Perfect Circuit

### Review Language — Pedal Interaction

- "It just sits in the mix perfectly" — common forum praise
- "Stacks well with other pedals" — standard review phrase
- "At unity gain it's like the pedal isn't on — then you work it from there" — TDPRI forum
- "Transparent and clean" — transparent overdrive standard language
- "Colors your tone" — forum language for non-transparent effects

## 11. ANALOG SYNTHESIZERS

### Oscillators — Where Everything Starts

- "Waveforms are the starting point of synthesis — and of all sound" — Perfect Circuit
- "At the heart of every synthesizer lies its oscillators, the fundamental sound generators that transform electrical energy into the raw waveforms we sculpt into music" — Home Studio Guys
- **Sine wave:** "The simplest waves — pure, fundamental frequencies" — Perfect Circuit
- **Square wave:** "Beepy and boopy computery noises — many harmonics, much brighter, sharper timbre" — Perfect Circuit
- **Triangle wave:** "A compromise — the smoothest one can get while preserving the computery nature" — Perfect Circuit
- **Sawtooth wave:** "The buzziest sounding of them all, even harsher than a square wave — the richest in terms of harmonics" — Perfect Circuit

### Filters — Removing What You Don't Need

- "A filter is used to remove harmonic content from a sound" — FabFilter
- "Filters shape sound by controlling which frequencies pass through and which are removed or attenuated" — various
- "The cutoff frequency is the point at which a filter starts to work" — Opto Productions
- "Resonance provides an emphasis to the cutoff frequency itself, often resulting in the filter having a more wet sound" — various
- "High resonance with a low-pass filter can create a whistling or squelchy sound" — various
- "Automating a low-pass filter on a pad can make it slowly open up, starting as a warm, muffled sound and gradually becoming brighter and more intense" — Molten Music Technology
- "The most iconic filter sweep sounds come from sweeping the cutoff frequency of a resonant lowpass filter" — Perfect Circuit

### ADSR Envelopes — How A Sound Lives And Dies

- "Attack, Decay, Sustain, Release — the four stages of the envelope, charting a sound's course from beginning to end" — Sound on Sound
- "An ADSR envelope determines how a sound unfolds over time" — Native Instruments
- "Envelopes help give shape to a sound by changing certain parameters from moment to moment" — various
- **Attack:** "The time taken for the signal to grow to its maximum amplitude, initiated by the pressing of a key" — Sound on Sound
- **Decay:** "After the attack phase the envelope immediately enters the Decay phase during which time the signal level reduces" — Sound on Sound
- **Sustain:** "The steady amplitude level produced when a key is held down" — MasterClass
- **Release:** "The final phase — determines the speed at which a sound ends from the moment you release" — Sound on Sound
- "Controls how a sound behaves over time, shaping everything from punchy drum hits to smooth evolving pads" — Point Blank

### LFO — The Invisible Wave

- "An LFO is a secondary oscillator that operates at a significantly lower frequency — below the range of human hearing" — Native Instruments
- "Produces an oscillating signal that's useless as an audio source since we can't hear it. However, this signal can be assigned to modulate other parameters" — various
- "Can be imagined as a tiny, invisible force pushing and pulling your music, creating mesmerizing wobbles and pulsations" — EDP Prod
- "When linked to multiple parameters of a sound, LFOs can give the impression that the sound is alive" — LANDR
- "Because the LFO movement is so slow, this creates a sweeping, pulsing effect" — LANDR
