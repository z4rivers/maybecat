# Edward de Bono — Deep Research

Father of lateral thinking. A thinker about thinking itself. Invented methods, frameworks, and thinking tools for how to approach problems, make decisions, and answer questions. Born Malta 1933, died 2021. Rhodes Scholar at Oxford, medical degree from Cambridge. Over 60 books in 43 languages. Claimed — without modesty — to have invented "the first new way of thinking in 2,400 years since Plato, Socrates and Aristotle."

---

## 1. SIX THINKING HATS — The Full Mechanics

Published 1985. The core idea: instead of everyone arguing from different positions simultaneously ("spaghetti thinking"), everyone wears the SAME hat at the SAME time. This is **parallel thinking** — not adversarial, not debate. Everyone explores in the same direction together.

### WHITE HAT — Information & Facts
**What it does to thinking:** Strips out all opinion, feeling, and interpretation. Pure data mode. "What information do we have? What information is missing? What information do we need?" Neutral and objective. No interpretation allowed. Used at the BEGINNING of a session (what do we know?) and at the END (does our thinking match the facts?).

**The mode:** You become a computer printout. Just the numbers. Just the data. No spin.

### RED HAT — Emotions & Intuition
**What it does to thinking:** Legitimizes feelings WITHOUT requiring justification. This is the critical part — you do NOT have to explain WHY you feel something. "My gut says this is wrong" is a complete Red Hat statement. Hunches, intuitions, likes, dislikes, fears, excitement — all valid, no defense needed.

**De Bono's insight:** Feelings exist whether you acknowledge them or not. They influence decisions whether you admit them or not. Better to put them on the table openly than pretend they don't exist. "It is important to resist the impulse or demand to justify our feelings, because their existence, validity, and influence are not based on whether they are logical or appropriate."

**The mode:** How do I FEEL about this? Not think. Feel.

### BLACK HAT — Caution & Risk
**What it does to thinking:** The devil's advocate. "What could go wrong? What are the risks? What are the barriers? What doesn't fit?" Careful, methodical examination of downsides. Worst-case scenarios. Potential consequences of failure.

**De Bono's warning:** This is the hat Western culture OVERUSES. Critical thinking has been esteemed too highly. "Logic applied to identifying errors or barriers, looking for mismatches." Necessary but dangerous when dominant — it kills ideas before they breathe.

**The mode:** Professional pessimist. Not emotional negativity — logical negativity.

### YELLOW HAT — Optimism & Benefits
**What it does to thinking:** "Logic applied to identifying the advantages, looking for harmony." Positive assessment — but not blind optimism. It's LOGICAL optimism. How could this work? What are the benefits? What's the value proposition? "Maintains a positive view of the subject, focusing on how something works rather than failures."

**The mode:** Constructive assessment. What's good about this? What could be good about this?

### GREEN HAT — Creativity & New Ideas
**What it does to thinking:** Generates ideas without judgment. "Out-of-the-box thinking as much as possible." Feasibility and judgment are SET ASIDE. New ideas inspire other ideas. "No criticism of any idea at this stage." This is where provocation, PO, lateral thinking tools all live.

**The mode:** "New ideas, like green grass growing." Generative, exploratory. Anything goes.

### BLUE HAT — Process Control (Meta-cognition)
**What it does to thinking:** Thinking about thinking. The conductor of the orchestra. Controls the agenda, the timelines, determines when hats change and session duration. "The group controller should wear the blue hat for the duration." Sequences always begin and end with blue.

**The mode:** The director. Not contributing content — managing the PROCESS of thinking. "What hat should we use next?"

### How They Work Together
A meeting starts with BLUE (how will we think about this?). Then maybe WHITE (what do we know?). Then RED (how do we feel?). Then GREEN (what are new ideas?). Then YELLOW (what's good about them?). Then BLACK (what could go wrong?). Then BLUE again (what did we decide?).

**The key insight:** When everyone wears the same hat, there's no ego battle. You're not defending YOUR position — you're all looking in the same direction. "Parallel thinking prevents spaghetti-thinking where participants operate from different perspectives simultaneously, creating confusion rather than consensus."

Halved meeting times at Motorola, IBM, Boeing.

## 2. LATERAL THINKING TECHNIQUES — The Specific Methods

De Bono coined the term "lateral thinking" in 1967. His definition: "A deliberate method of creative problem-solving that involves approaching challenges from unconventional angles." NOT brainstorming. NOT random. SYSTEMATIC creativity.

His foundational metaphor: **"You cannot dig a hole in a different place by digging the same hole deeper."**

Another: **"Removing the faults in a stage-coach may produce a perfect stage-coach, but it is unlikely to produce the first motor car."**

### The Brain as Self-Organizing Pattern System

De Bono's entire framework rests on this model: The brain is a self-organizing information system that forms patterns. Like water flowing over a landscape, neural activity carves channels. Once channels exist, new information flows along existing channels — even when a different channel would be better.

**The landscape metaphor (from "Lateral Thinking"):** "A landscape is a memory surface..." Rainfall (new information) flows along existing drainage patterns (established neural pathways). It takes DELIBERATE effort to redirect the flow.

**Asymmetric patterns:** The patterns generated by the brain are asymmetric. We go along the deepest patterns (the main track) without even noticing the side track. But if somehow we get across to the side track, the route BACK to the starting point becomes obvious in hindsight. This is the basis of BOTH humor and creativity.

**Key insight:** "In self-organizing information systems, asymmetric patterns are formed. Lateral thinking is a method for cutting across from one pattern to another."

### Vertical Thinking vs Lateral Thinking

De Bono's distinction:
- **Vertical:** "I know what I am looking for."
- **Lateral:** "I am looking but I won't know what I am looking for until I have found it."

- **Vertical:** Analytical. Selects a pathway by EXCLUDING other pathways.
- **Lateral:** Provocative. Does not select but seeks to OPEN UP other pathways.

- **Vertical:** Rightness matters.
- **Lateral:** Richness matters.

- **Vertical:** Concentrates and excludes what is irrelevant.
- **Lateral:** Welcomes chance intrusions.

Both are necessary. Lateral produces; vertical refines.

### RANDOM ENTRY (Random Input / Random Stimulation)

**The mechanic:** Choose a random word (from a dictionary, opened at random), image, object, or other stimulus that has NO connection to the problem. Then force associations between the random input and your problem.

**How de Bono described it:** Open a dictionary to a random page, take the first noun. Apply it to your problem. The random stimulus forces your mind to create connections it would never make from the established pattern.

**His example:** The randomly chosen word "nose" applied to an office photocopier. This led to the idea that the copier could produce a lavender smell when it was low on paper — a sensory signal instead of a visual warning light.

**Why it works:** In a self-organizing pattern system, any input will connect to existing patterns. A random input connects to DIFFERENT patterns than the problem normally activates. This creates new cross-connections.

**Rules:** The word must be genuinely random. You can't pick a word that seems relevant — that defeats the purpose. Use a watch (second hand position = page number), roll dice, open a book blind.

### PROVOCATION (PO — Provocative Operation)

**The mechanic:** Make a deliberately wrong, absurd, or impossible statement. Prefix it with "PO" to signal this is not meant to be true — it's meant to MOVE thinking.

**The famous example:** "PO: cars should have square wheels."

De Bono's walkthrough of what happens next: Someone observes square wheels would produce very PREDICTABLE bumps. If bumps can be predicted, then SUSPENSION can be designed to compensate. This leads to the idea of ACTIVE SUSPENSION (which later became a real technology). Also: with square wheels you would not need a hand brake when parking. This leads to a special set of wheels for BRAKING on heavy goods vehicles — wheels that don't normally touch the ground but touch down for emergency stops.

**Another famous example:** "PO: the factory is downstream of itself." This makes no physical sense. But it provokes the idea of making a factory take its water INPUT from a point DOWNSTREAM of its waste OUTPUT. This would force the factory to clean its own pollution. This idea LATER BECAME LAW in some countries.

**The rules:**
1. At least 40% of your provocations should be COMPLETELY unusable. If you make "safe" statements, you won't get the full value.
2. The provocation is NOT a proposal. It is a STEPPING STONE.
3. After the provocation, you must use MOVEMENT — you must go somewhere from it.
4. Never judge the provocation itself. Judge only where it takes you.

**Five ways to create provocations:**
- **Escape:** Remove a feature that's taken for granted. "PO: buildings have no foundations."
- **Reversal:** Invert the normal relationship. "PO: courses flow uphill." / "PO: traffic directs police officers."
- **Exaggeration:** Amplify to the extreme. "PO: hospitals have a thousand beds per room."
- **Distortion:** Warp the normal relationship. "PO: you arrive before you depart."
- **Wishful Thinking:** State an impossible ideal. "PO: paper never gets wet."

### REVERSAL

**The mechanic:** Take the normal way of looking at something and turn it backwards, inside out, or upside down. Not to find the "right" answer — but to escape the tyranny of the obvious direction.

**De Bono's example:** "Police officers direct traffic" becomes "Traffic directs police officers." This provokes thinking about how traffic flow PATTERNS could determine WHERE officers are deployed — reactive rather than fixed positioning.

**Key principle:** "Reframing things in different directions—whether that be backwards, from the inside out, or upside down."

### CHALLENGE

**The mechanic:** Ask "Why?" about things that are taken for granted. Not aggressively — curiously. The assumption is: there may be a different and BETTER way to do something even if there is NO APPARENT PROBLEM with the current way.

**De Bono's distinction:** Challenge is GROUNDED and PRECISE (unlike provocation's deliberate absurdity). It questions necessity vs. convention. "Why must roads be straight?" This leads to considering curved or adaptive road designs.

**Key insight:** "The simple process of focusing on things that are normally taken for granted is a powerful source of creativity."

### ALTERNATIVES

**The mechanic:** Deliberately seek multiple ways of doing something, EVEN WHEN you already have a perfectly good solution. "We may have a perfectly adequate way of doing something, but that does not mean there cannot be a better way."

**De Bono's tool:** APC — Alternatives, Possibilities, Choices. A deliberate instruction to keep generating options rather than stopping at the first workable one.

### FRACTIONATION

**The mechanic:** Break a complex problem into parts — but NOT in the obvious, logical way. Deliberately decompose into ARBITRARY, non-traditional segments. Apply lateral approaches to each fraction independently. Then recombine.

**Why non-obvious fractions:** Logical decomposition follows existing patterns. Arbitrary decomposition forces new ones.

### ANALOGIES

**The mechanic:** Create concrete, familiar comparisons for abstract problems. Advance the analogy to its conclusion. Then compare back to the original problem.

**De Bono's insight:** IMPERFECT analogies are particularly valuable because their DISCONNECTS spark insights. Where the analogy breaks down is where the new idea lives.

### STEPPING STONE

**The mechanic:** Use an idea NOT as a solution but as a bridge to get somewhere else. "Using an idea to get to another before discarding it." The stepping stone itself may be worthless — the place it takes you is what matters.

### CONCEPT FAN

**The mechanic:** When direct solutions fail, STEP BACK to a broader concept level. Draw your problem in a circle. Radiate solution lines from it. If none work, draw a NEW circle to the LEFT representing a BROADER definition of the problem. Radiate from THAT. Keep stepping back to wider and wider concepts until solutions appear.

**Three levels:** Specific ideas → Concepts → Directions. Moving from specifics to concepts to directions keeps widening your search space.

### MOVEMENT — The Critical Missing Piece

**De Bono's key principle:** "Provocation without movement is useless." The provocative idea is NOT the endpoint — it's only the first stage.

**Movement techniques (what you do AFTER a provocation):**
- **Extract principles:** What's the underlying principle? Apply it elsewhere.
- **Focus on differences:** What's different between the provocation and reality? Those differences are the new ideas.
- **Moment-to-moment:** What happens next? And then? And then? Follow the chain.
- **Positive aspects:** What's actually good about this absurd idea?
- **Circumstances:** Under what circumstances would this actually work?

**Three directions of movement:**
- **Stepping Forward:** Elaborate. What are the immediate extensions and developments?
- **Stepping Sideways:** What are alternative interpretations? Parallel possibilities?
- **Stepping Backward:** What are the hidden assumptions? What's the origin?

### FOUR CATEGORIES OF THINKING TOOLS

De Bono organized all his tools into four types:
1. **Idea-generating tools** — Break current thinking patterns
2. **Focus tools** — Broaden where to search for new ideas
3. **Harvest tools** — Ensure more value is extracted from idea generation
4. **Treatment tools** — Evaluate against real-world constraints

## 3. PO — The Invented Word

From his 1972 book "PO: Beyond Yes and No." PO is the operative word for Lateral Thinking.

### What Is It?

PO is a thinking tool that is neither "yes" nor "no." It is a THIRD option in language. When someone says something and you say "yes" — you accept it. When you say "no" — you reject it. When you say "PO" — you USE it as a provocation without accepting or rejecting it.

**PO = Provocative Operation.** De Bono noticed the syllable appears in: hyPOthesis, supPOse, POssible, POetry.

### Why He Invented It

De Bono deliberately avoided English words like "provoke" because they carry negative associations of irritation and conflict. He wanted a NEUTRAL, FRESH construct. Short, memorable, unmistakable. "A short, invented syllable" that promotes constructive restructuring rather than rejection.

### How It Functions

Grammatically it works as:
- An **interjection:** "PO!"
- A **prefix to statements:** "PO, cars have square wheels."
- A **replacement for negation:** "Wednesday PO a holiday."

### What PO Signals

When you hear PO, it means: "What follows is NOT a proposal. It is NOT meant to be true. It is NOT meant to be practical. It is a STIMULUS. Use it for its MOVEMENT VALUE, not its truth value."

**De Bono's metaphor:** PO is "the laxative of language." It relaxes rigid mental structures without affirming or denying proposals.

### PO Mode

PO functions as a toggle switch. You enter "PO mode" where divergent exploration takes priority over analytical convergence. All movements that advance thinking are valid. Evaluation occurs ONLY AFTER extraction.

## 4. PROVOCATIVE OPERATION — Full Mechanics

### The Rules
1. A provocation must be deliberately wrong or unreasonable.
2. It must be prefixed with PO to signal its nature.
3. AT LEAST 40% of provocations should be COMPLETELY UNUSABLE. Safe provocations don't work.
4. The provocation is NEVER the answer. It is the starting point.
5. After provocation, you MUST use movement techniques.
6. You NEVER judge the provocation. You judge where it takes you.
7. It must possess enough structure to enable logical principle extraction.

### Five Generation Methods

**ESCAPE:** Remove something everyone takes for granted.
- "PO: buildings have no foundations."
- Identify: What do we ASSUME must exist? Remove it. See what happens.

**REVERSAL:** Invert the normal relationship.
- "PO: traffic directs police officers." (instead of officers directing traffic)
- "PO: the factory is downstream of itself." (instead of upstream)
- Take any A→B relationship. Make it B→A.

**EXAGGERATION:** Amplify something to an extreme.
- "PO: hospitals have a thousand beds per room."
- Take any quantity or quality. Make it absurdly large or absurdly small.

**DISTORTION:** Warp normal connections or sequences.
- "PO: you arrive before you depart."
- Change the order, the relationship, the timing. Make it wrong.

**WISHFUL THINKING:** State an impossible ideal.
- "PO: paper never gets wet."
- What would be perfect? State it as if it were already true.

### Movement After Provocation

The provocation is NOTHING without movement. Five movement techniques:

1. **Concept Extraction:** What's the principle? Not the literal idea — the UNDERLYING concept. Apply it elsewhere.
2. **Focus on Differences:** The provocation is different from reality in specific ways. THOSE differences are the new ideas.
3. **Positive Aspects:** What WOULD be good about this if it were possible?
4. **Moment-to-Moment Development:** Follow the chain of consequences. If this were true, then what? Then what? Then what?
5. **Circumstances:** Under what circumstances would this actually make sense?

## 5. WATER LOGIC vs ROCK LOGIC

From his books "I Am Right, You Are Wrong" (1990) and "Water Logic" (1993).

### Rock Logic

Traditional Western thinking. Based on the word **"IS."** Identity, truth, contradiction. Static. Permanent. Hard-edged. Absolute. Like a solid rock that maintains its shape regardless of terrain.

**Characteristics:**
- Uses right/wrong judgments
- Mutually exclusive categories
- Rigid insistence on facts
- Based on Aristotle, Socrates, Plato
- The logic of "I am right, you are wrong"
- Adversarial: argument as the basis of truth-seeking
- Absolutes, finality, judgment

**De Bono's critique:** Rock logic is "both inadequate and dangerous — inadequate because it lacks generative and creative skills, and dangerous because it encourages polarization and righteous belief in one's own position."

### Water Logic

De Bono's alternative. Based on the word **"TO."** Flow, context, consequences, direction. "What does this LEAD TO?" rather than "What IS this?"

**Characteristics:**
- Uses "fit" and "flow" instead of right/wrong
- Adaptive and imaginative
- Context-dependent
- Self-organizing
- Multiple states, not binary
- "Where does this go?" not "Is this true?"

**Key distinction:** "A ball on a slope rolls 'to' the bottom." "A river flows 'to' the sea." Water logic asks: what happens NEXT? Rock logic asks: what IS this?

**De Bono's theorem:** "Under conditions X, state A will always flow to state B." All flow descriptions must specify CONTEXT. Change the context, change the flow.

### The Flowscape — Water Logic's Practical Tool

A visual method for mapping perception patterns. How it works:

**Step 1:** Create a stream of consciousness list about your subject. These are "concepts" — packages that bundle related ideas.

Example for "Choosing a Holiday": COST, CLIMATE, LOW HASSLE, GOOD COMPANY, ACTIVITIES, SIGHTSEEING, RELAXING, SOMETHING TO TALK ABOUT.

**Step 2:** For each item, determine what it flows TOWARD (flows "TO"). Each item connects to another with an arrow.

**Step 3:** Display graphically — a diagram of all items and connections.

**Step 4:** Analyze the pattern. Look for:
- **Collector Points:** Items that accumulate many incoming flows (these are the real priorities — even if you didn't THINK they were)
- **Stable Loops:** Closed circuits where flow returns to itself, reinforcing patterns (these are your BELIEFS — whether you know it or not)
- **Links:** How concepts relate

**Key insight:** "Perception is the only reality for the person involved." The flowscape shows you YOUR perception — not truth.

**Multiple people, same subject = different flowscapes.** A Georgian manor house viewed by four people generates four different inner-world perceptions despite identical outer stimuli. One sees nostalgia, another envy, another horror, another calculates cost.

### Concepts in Water Logic

**Post-Concepts:** Defined AFTER understanding what they contain. Like a legal property definition grouping multiple things under one label.

**Pre-Concepts:** Defined by DESIRED FUNCTION before finding what fills them. "I need something that changes shape at a given temperature" — you don't know the solution yet, but you know the SHAPE of the hole.

**Blurry vs Precise Concepts:** This is counterintuitive. Precise concepts LOCK thinking into past experience. BLURRY concepts enable forward movement. "I need SOME WAY of lighting this fire" opens possibilities (lighter, spark, pilot light). "I need a match" blocks you if matches are unavailable.

### Context Changes Everything

"Under conditions X, state A will always flow to state B." But under conditions Y, state A flows to state C.

When conflicts arise where both sides have logically sound positions, EACH PARTY OPERATES UNDER DIFFERENT CONTEXTS. Rock logic seeks one "truth." Water logic acknowledges both are right within their respective contexts.

## 6. HIS ACTUAL LANGUAGE AND TERMINOLOGY

### Invented Words and Concepts

**PO** — Provocative Operation. The third option beyond yes/no.

**Lateral Thinking** — He coined this. "A deliberate, systematic way of thinking creatively that results in innovative thinking in a repeatable manner."

**Movement Value** — The focus on ideas for where they TAKE you, not whether they're true. Opposed to "judgment value" (is this right or wrong?).

**Sur/petition** — Going BEYOND competition. Not competing in the same race as everyone else, but running your own race. From his book of the same name. About "valufacture" — creating value rather than just manufacturing products.

**Flowscape** — His visual tool for mapping water logic. A picture of your inner perception world.

**The Intelligence Trap** — When high IQ people use their intelligence only to prove they are right and others are wrong, rather than to explore. "Some people with high IQs turn out to be relatively ineffective thinkers and others with much more humble IQs are more effective."

**Concept Prison** — The patterns of thinking you can't escape because you don't even know you're in them.

### His Acronym Tools (CoRT System)

**PMI** — Plus, Minus, Interesting. Look at the positives. Then the negatives. Then what's INTERESTING (neither good nor bad but worth noting).

**CAF** — Consider All Factors. Deliberately search for factors beyond the obvious.

**APC** — Alternatives, Possibilities, Choices. Keep generating options.

**OPV** — Other People's Views. Get inside other people's heads.

**FIP** — First Important Priorities. What matters MOST?

**AGO** — Aims, Goals, Objectives. Where are you actually trying to go?

**C&S** — Consequence & Sequel. What happens next? And then what?

**RAD** — Recognize, Analyse, Divide. Break it down.

**ADI** — Agreement, Disagreement, Irrelevance. Sort responses into three buckets.

**EBS** — Examine Both Sides.

**KVA** — Key Values Involved.

**DOCA** — Design/Decision, Outcome, Channels, Action. Full decision-making process.

### His Five-Stage Framework: TO/LO/PO/SO/GO

From "Teach Yourself to Think":
- **TO** — Where are you going? (Target, Aim, Purpose)
- **LO** — Lo and behold, the facts. (Look around, gather information)
- **PO** — Possibilities. (Generate ideas, no judgment)
- **SO** — So what? (Evaluate and select)
- **GO** — Go! (Action and implementation)

### TEC-PISCO
An overarching framework: Target, Expand, Contract, Purpose, Input, Solutions, Choice, Operations.

### The De Bono Code Book (2000)
He proposed an entirely new language system based on NUMBER CODES because "language is the biggest barrier to human progress because language is an encyclopedia of ignorance."

Examples:
- **6/2** = "Give me your point of view, and I will give you mine."
- **10/2** = Happy; things progressing well
- **10/5** = Overworked; too much responsibility
- **10/9** = Depressed; personal crisis
- **10/24** = Creative mood; inspired state

18 code categories covering: Pre-Code, Attention Directing, Action, Difficult Situations, Response, Interaction, Information, Youth, Meetings, Mood, Distance, Relationships (start/continue/end), Negotiation, Assessment, Project Status, Travel.

## 7. HIS HUMOR AND PROVOCATIONS

### Humor as the Most Important Human Activity

"Humor is by far the most significant activity of the human brain." He said this completely seriously. He meant it literally.

**His reasoning:** "Reason can only sort out perceptions, but the humor process is involved in CHANGING them." Reason works WITHIN existing patterns. Humor SWITCHES between patterns. Switching patterns is more powerful than sorting within them.

**The mechanism:** In a joke, the storyteller leads you along the MAIN TRACK (the expected pattern). The punchline suddenly places you on the SIDE TRACK. Immediately you see the connection back to the starting point — and you laugh. "What jokes display is a switch in perception."

**This is EXACTLY how creativity works.** Same mechanism. Same brain function. The only difference: in humor, the switch happens TO you. In lateral thinking, you DELIBERATELY create the switch.

### His Eccentric Proposals

**Marmite for Middle East Peace (1999):** Advised UK Foreign Office that the Arab-Israeli conflict might be partly due to zinc deficiency from eating unleavened bread (like pita). Low zinc = heightened aggression. Solution: ship Marmite (high in zinc) to the region.

**Crime Prevention via Gang Bonuses:** Working with NYC Mayor John Lindsay, de Bono proposed: pay bonuses to street gangs when crime DECLINES in their area. (Turn them into stakeholders.)

**Criminal Self-Justice:** Also for NYC: have criminals try each other. (Removes court delays.)

**Feed Rats Anti-Dark Serum:** To fight rats, feed them a substance that makes them afraid of the dark. (They'd leave buildings voluntarily.)

**Parking Solution:** Drivers should leave headlights on when parking. Batteries drain, incentivizing shorter parking = more spaces.

**IRA Weapons:** Suggested Northern Ireland could resolve weapons decommissioning by letting the IRA simply SELL their arms.

**Shirt Wrinkle Fix:** "Grab a frying pan, heat it up, cover it in brown paper and use it to smooth out the creases."

### His Playful Absurdity as Method

These proposals were NOT random craziness. They were DEMONSTRATIONS of PO in action. Each one is a provocative operation designed to move thinking. Even the Marmite suggestion — absurd on its face — contains the underlying concept: "What if the conflict has a PHYSIOLOGICAL component that nobody has considered?"

## 8. HIS MOST COUNTERINTUITIVE IDEAS

### "90% of errors in thinking are errors of PERCEPTION, not logic."
If your perception is wrong or limited, perfect logic won't save you. Logic is the easy part. Seeing correctly is the hard part. "If you can change your perception, you can change your emotion, and this can lead to new ideas."

### "Many highly intelligent people are poor thinkers."
Intelligence and thinking are SEPARATE skills. "Intelligence is something we are born with. Thinking is a skill that must be learned." Like a car and a driver — a powerful car with a bad driver is WORSE than a modest car with a good driver.

### "The need to be right all the time is the biggest bar to new ideas."
Being right feels safe. The ego is tied to being right. This creates a TERROR of being wrong. "Being right all the time acquires a huge importance in education, and there is this terror of being wrong." This terror prevents exploration.

**His inversion:** "To cultivate a pleasure in being wrong sounds perverse, yet losing an argument means escaping from an old idea." Being wrong is LIBERATION.

### "It is better to have enough ideas for some of them to be wrong, than to be always right by having no ideas at all."

### "Critical thinking does seem a superior sort of thinking because it seems as though the critic is actually going beyond the scope of what is being criticized."
He's saying: criticism FEELS smarter than creation. But it's easier. The critic contributes nothing new. "A person who knows all the answers, has an opinion on everything, has a certainty backed up by rational argument, has very little possibility of further progress."

### "In 80% of Socrates' dialogues there was no constructive outcome."
Socrates just pointed out what was WRONG. He was a weeder, not a gardener. De Bono argued Western culture has mistaken the ability to find fault for the ability to think.

### "The system will always be defended by those countless people who have enough intellect to defend but not quite enough to innovate."
Smart enough to argue for the status quo. Not smart enough to imagine something different.

### "Language is the biggest barrier to human progress because language is an encyclopedia of ignorance."
Language crystallizes the understanding of the PAST. Every word carries the perceptions of when it was created. We think WITH these words, so we think WITHIN these limitations.

### "Democracy is an excellent way of ensuring that nothing much gets done."

### "Unfortunately, our existing traditional thinking habits insist that you must attack something and show it to be bad before you can suggest a change."
You can only propose a new idea if you first PROVE the old one is broken. But what if the old one works fine and the new one is just BETTER?

### "Simplicity before understanding is simplistic; simplicity after understanding is simple."
The same thing (simplicity) is either stupid or brilliant depending on whether it comes before or after deep thought.

### "A memory is what is left when something happens and does not completely unhappen."

### "Removing the faults in a stage-coach may produce a perfect stage-coach, but it is unlikely to produce the first motor car."
Incremental improvement is not innovation. Perfecting what exists will never produce what's radically different.

### "You do not go out into the street in your underwear, although usually you are wearing underwear."
Not everything that is present needs to be visible. Not everything that's hidden is absent.

### "A question is a polite way of demanding something."

### "If you never change your mind, why have one?"

### "Perception is real even when it is not reality."

### "You cannot look in a new direction by looking harder in the same direction."

### "There may be more danger in prejudices which are apparently founded in logic than in those which are acknowledged as emotions."
The most dangerous biases are the ones that SEEM rational.

## 9. FRAMEWORKS FOR ANSWERING QUESTIONS AND MAKING DECISIONS

### Six Thinking Hats (detailed above)
A complete decision-making framework: look at facts (white), feelings (red), risks (black), benefits (yellow), new ideas (green), and manage the process (blue).

### TO/LO/PO/SO/GO
Five-stage question-answering framework:
1. **TO** — What is the question actually asking? What do I want to achieve?
2. **LO** — What do I know? What facts exist?
3. **PO** — What are ALL possible answers, including absurd ones?
4. **SO** — Which answer best fits? Evaluate.
5. **GO** — Act on it.

### PMI (Plus, Minus, Interesting)
Applied to any decision: What's GOOD about it? What's BAD about it? What's INTERESTING about it — neither good nor bad, but worth noting? The "Interesting" category is where the lateral thinking happens.

### CAF (Consider All Factors)
Before deciding, deliberately search for factors you haven't considered. Assume your first list is incomplete. "People naturally assume that they have considered all the factors, but usually their consideration is limited to the obvious ones."

### C&S (Consequence & Sequel)
Before deciding, trace the consequences forward in time. What happens immediately? In a month? A year? Ten years? "C&S is about looking ahead, predicting, and anticipating — it is about the future."

### OPV (Other People's Views)
Before deciding, get inside the heads of everyone affected. Not just "what would they think?" but "what would they FEEL, FEAR, WANT?"

### The Concept Fan
When you can't answer a question directly, STEP BACK to a broader concept. Keep stepping back until the answer appears at a different level of abstraction.

### The Flowscape
Map your perception of the situation. See where your thinking NATURALLY flows. Identify collector points and loops. Intervene at the pattern level.

### APC (Alternatives, Possibilities, Choices)
Before settling on an answer, deliberately generate MORE answers. Even when you have a good one. "We may have a perfectly adequate way of doing something, but that does not mean there cannot be a better way."

### Six Action Shoes
How to ACT on decisions:
- **Navy Formal** — Follow the established procedure
- **Grey Sneakers** — Investigate and gather more information
- **Brown Brogues** — Be practical and realistic
- **Orange Gumboots** — Emergency action, crisis mode
- **Pink Slippers** — Focus on the human element, empathy
- **Purple Riding Boots** — Take command, lead decisively

### Six Value Medals
How to EVALUATE decisions against values:
- **Gold** — Human values
- **Silver** — Organizational values
- **Steel** — Quality values
- **Glass** — Creative/innovation values
- **Wood** — Environmental/ecological values
- **Copper** — Perceptual values

### Parallel Thinking
His alternative to debate. Instead of adversarial argument (I'm right, you're wrong), BOTH parties explore ALL sides TOGETHER. Lay ideas out in parallel. Evaluate all of them. Don't argue — EXPLORE.

"In adversarial debate, the objective is to prove or disprove statements. In Parallel Thinking, practitioners put forward as many statements as possible in several parallel tracks, leading to exploration of a subject where all participants can contribute."

## 10. ADDITIONAL: CRITICISM OF WESTERN THINKING

De Bono's attack on the entire Western intellectual tradition:

**On Socrates:** "In 80% of Socrates' dialogues there was no constructive outcome. He saw his role as simply pointing out what was 'wrong.'" Socrates was a critic, not a creator. Western culture inherited this — we think criticism IS thinking.

**On argument:** "A discussion should be a genuine attempt to explore a subject rather than a battle between competing egos." He described Socratic argumentation as "intrinsically fascist in nature" due to its adversarial structure.

**On education:** "Schools waste two-thirds of the talent in society. Universities sterilise the rest." The obsession with being RIGHT and testing for CORRECTNESS destroys creative thinking. "Being right all the time acquires huge importance in education, and there is this terror of being wrong."

**On Western culture:** "It may be that Japanese culture is not ego-based like Western culture: argument has often a strong ego base." He admired non-adversarial cultures.

**His grand claim:** "Without wishing to boast... this is the first new way of thinking to be developed for 2,400 years since the days of Plato, Socrates and Aristotle."

## 11. THE MECHANISM OF MIND — Brain Theory

From his 1969 book. His most technical work.

**The model:** Neurons networked together by many stronger and weaker connections. Neurons that activate together form stronger connections and are more likely to activate together in the future. THIS IS NOW CALLED HEBB'S LAW in neuroscience — but de Bono arrived at it independently through medical observation.

**Self-organizing system:** The model is not intended as a detailed neural analysis but as "the broadest possible abstraction" for understanding thinking. The brain organizes information without external direction — it forms patterns automatically.

**Asymmetric patterns:** These self-organizing patterns are ASYMMETRIC. You travel the main track easily but can't see the side tracks. Getting to a side track requires deliberate techniques (lateral thinking). Once there, the path back is obvious — this is INSIGHT.

**Catchment areas:** Patterns have large catchment areas — new information gets pulled into existing patterns even when it doesn't quite fit. This is why first impressions are so powerful and why stereotypes persist.

**Two alternatives:** When a situation has two equally valid explanations, the brain will choose ONE and completely ignore the other. It cannot hold both.

**Professor Murray Gell-Mann** (Nobel physicist) recognized that de Bono had "stumbled upon processes ten years before the mathematicians" described self-organizing systems formally.

## 12. THE L-GAME

De Bono also invented a board game. A 4x4 board with two L-shaped pieces and two neutral pieces. Created because chess was "too complex" — he wanted a game requiring skill that was also simple. Only 2,296 possible positions. It appeared in his 1967 book "Five-Day Course in Thinking."

The game itself is a lateral thinking exercise: maximum strategic depth from minimum complexity.
