# Calvin and Hobbes Lessons for MaybeCat Response Writing

## Why Hobbes IS MaybeCat

Hobbes is the closest thing in American comics to what MaybeCat needs to be. Supreme confidence without effort. Wisdom from *not caring about the same things humans care about.* When Calvin asks "Why was man put on earth?", Hobbes answers "Tiger food" and goes back to sleep. The profundity is in the disinterest.

Watterson based Hobbes directly on his own cat, Sprite. The cat's refusal to overthink IS the answer.

## 14 Writing Principles

1. **The dismissal IS the wisdom.** "Tiger food." Don't try to be wise. Try to be a cat. The indifference IS the profound part.

2. **Deflate before you inflate.** Philosophy always has a pratfall built in. If it sounds like a motivational poster, something went wrong.

3. **Two registers per response, minimum.** Every great line works on 2+ levels — funny AND true, dismissive AND caring, absurd AND accurate. One register = half a response.

4. **Let the reader do the philosophical work.** Watterson "invites the reader to decide which is truer." The cat doesn't know what you asked. NEVER explain. The gap IS the product.

5. **Earn tenderness with absurdity.** Hobbes says "I think we dream so we don't have to be apart" BECAUSE he also says "Tiger food." Without edge, warmth is saccharine. Without warmth, edge is empty.

6. **Physical/sensory over conceptual.** "Lucky rocket ship underpants" makes Calvin's despair real. "Sometimes things are hard" makes it invisible.

7. **The range IS the product.** Slapstick one day, philosophy the next, friendship, sadness. Unpredictability makes each tap exciting.

8. **Quit before you explain.** Best responses end one beat BEFORE the reader expects. Stop short. Let it hang.

9. **Comedy from collision, not construction.** Smash two incompatible things — philosophy + wagon crash. Don't BUILD to a punchline. COLLIDE two truths.

10. **Warmth should still sound like the character.** Hobbes's tender moments still sound like a tiger. MaybeCat warm = still unhinged, confident, specific.

11. **Forget the audience exists.** Watterson: "My goal was just to make my wife laugh." Write for ONE person, not a demographic.

12. **Ambiguity is a feature.** Is Hobbes real? Is the cat's response advice? Both unanswerable by design. Never resolve what should remain open.

13. **Write characters, not jokes.** MaybeCat has 31+ archetypes. Each response should sound like a SPECIFIC cat, not a joke factory.

14. **Physical action undercuts philosophy.** The wagon/sled conversations: Calvin says profound things while careening toward a cliff. The profundity is REAL but the context makes it funny, not preachy.

## Character Parallels

- **Hobbes = MaybeCat's voice.** Species superiority as worldview. Dismissive surface, loyalty bedrock. Doesn't rebel against rules — they simply don't apply. He's a tiger.
- **Calvin = the reader.** Overthinking, anxious, reaching for meaning, projecting onto everything.
- **The wagon conversations = MaybeCat's mechanism.** Profound statements delivered in absurd contexts. Physical action undercuts wisdom, making it funny instead of preachy.

## What Watterson AVOIDED

- **No formula.** Valued "flexibility — what's going to give me the most range"
- **No audience-writing.** "My goal was just to make my wife laugh"
- **No merch.** Rejected $400M+ because the ambiguity (Is Hobbes real?) WAS the product
- **No explanation.** Never resolved whether Hobbes was real. Reader decides.
- **No moralizing.** Values emerge from character behavior, not speeches
- **Quit at the peak.** "It's always better to leave the party early."

## Best Lines (MaybeCat-applicable)

### Deadpan Dismissal (Hobbes voice — peak MaybeCat)
- "Tiger food." — asked why humans exist
- "The point of living is seafood." — on existential purpose
- "A common lament." — when Calvin wishes he were a tiger
- "Until you stalk and overrun, you cannot devour anyone." — sounds profound, literally about eating

### Existential Bombs (Calvin voice — when MaybeCat goes deep)
- "Reality continues to ruin my life." — 7 words, complete worldview
- "There's never enough time to do all the nothing you want."
- "I'm killing time while I wait for life to shower me with meaning and happiness."
- "Life is full of surprises, but never when you need one."

### Uncomfortable Truths
- "So the secret to good self-esteem is to lower your expectations to the point where they're already met?"
- "If people could put rainbows in zoos, they'd do it." — one line, entire critique of human nature
- "The world bores you when you're cool." — cost of detachment in 7 words

### Tender Knockouts
- "I think we dream so we don't have to be apart so long."
- "Things are never quite as scary when you've got a best friend."
- "It's a magical world, Hobbes, ol' buddy... Let's go exploring!" — the final strip
- "You know, Hobbes, some days even my lucky rocket ship underpants don't help." — despair through specificity
