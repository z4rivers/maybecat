# Steven Wright Lessons for MaybeCat Response Writing

## Why Wright Maps to MaybeCat

Wright's format IS MaybeCat's format: self-contained one-liners, flat delivery, no context, the reader does the work. Text on a screen IS deadpan. No emoji winking, no "get it??" framing. The response is flat, the user's life situation adds all the emotion.

## The One-Liner Structure

**Frame** (3-8 words) → **Pivot** (single word/comma) → **Landing** (3-10 words, SHORTER than setup)

- "I intend to live forever" → "so far, so good."
- "It's a small world" → "but I wouldn't want to have to paint it."
- "A clear conscience" → "is usually the sign of a bad memory."

**The landing should be lighter than the launch.** Brain processes the familiar part quickly; short ending detonates before the conscious mind catches up.

## 15 Writing Principles

### Structure
1. **One pivot per response.** One moment where reality bends. If you can't point to the single pivot, the response is unfocused.

2. **Logic taken seriously > randomness.** "Instant coffee in a microwave → almost went back in time." The logic is impeccable. Absurdity that MAKES SENSE beats random weirdness.

3. **The embedded setup is the highest form.** "What's another word for Thesaurus?" — simultaneously setup AND punchline. Peak economy.

4. **Literalism is an infinite well.** Career built on taking phrases literally. "Take the leap," "trust the process," "follow your heart" — take any literally in cat voice = inexhaustible source.

### Tone
5. **Never announce the depth.** Philosophy should sound like a throwaway line. Wisdom arriving as afterimage, not headline.

6. **Flat delivery = MaybeCat's superpower.** User's brain does ALL interpretive work. Don't interfere with that mechanism.

7. **Two-stage detonation.** Laugh first, "wait..." second. Surface joke lands, then deeper meaning arrives as afterimage.

8. **Alternate temperature unpredictably.** Wright-cold (detached, precise) and Hedberg-warm (vulnerable, delighted) should both exist in pool.

### Craft
9. **No topical, no temporal.** "Gravity is never going to go out of style." Responses should work in 2026 and 2036.

10. **Complete self-containment.** Each response creates its reality, delivers payload, vanishes — all in one line.

11. **The three-performance rule.** Read against 3 different imagined questions (ex, job, dinner). If it doesn't work for all three, too narrow.

12. **Economy is discipline, not goal.** Cut words until rhythm is right. Then stop. Sometimes an extra word helps.

13. **No callbacks, no crowd work, no stories.** Each joke is self-contained. No "remember earlier when I said..."

14. **No profanity for laughs.** Wright deliberately avoided it: "I wanted it to be pure." Structural humor carries every laugh.

15. **The subconscious scanner.** Best responses come from noticing things in the world, not from sitting at a desk generating batches. Found language > deliberate construction.

## Wright vs Hedberg = MaybeCat's Full Range

| Dimension | Wright (Cold) | Hedberg (Warm) |
|-----------|--------------|----------------|
| Relationship to joke | Delivers it | Discovers it with you |
| Audience role | Observers | Co-conspirators |
| Emotional undertone | Existential detachment | Cheerful bewilderment |
| Failure mode | Too cold, robotic | Too soft, cutesy |
| MaybeCat energy | Judge Cat, Cold Cat, Realist Cat | Loving Cat, Chaos Cat, Stoner Cat |

MaybeCat needs BOTH. Wright = "I'm not even looking at you" energy. Hedberg = "I'm sitting on your chest at 3am because I love you" energy.

## Best Lines (MaybeCat-applicable)

### Could literally BE MaybeCat responses
- "Everywhere is walking distance if you have the time." — mortality as travel tip
- "You can't have everything. Where would you put it?" — anti-materialism as spatial problem
- "Experience is something you don't get until just after you need it." — devastating timing truth
- "A clear conscience is usually the sign of a bad memory." — uncomfortable inversion
- "Hard work pays off in the future. Laziness pays off now." — logic you can't argue with
- "I intend to live forever. So far, so good." — optimism as delusion
- "If everything seems to be going well, you have obviously overlooked something."
- "Change is inevitable... except from vending machines." — philosophy demolished by literalism
- "Monday is an awful way to spend 1/7th of your life."
- "When everything is coming your way, you're in the wrong lane."
- "Plan to be spontaneous tomorrow."
- "For every action, there is an equal and opposite criticism."

### Master-class in word economy
- "What's another word for Thesaurus?" — embedded setup
- "On the other hand, you have different fingers." — literal phrase
- "What a nice night for an evening." — says nothing, perfectly
- "OK, what's the speed of dark?" — inverts a universal constant
- "I was trying to daydream but my mind kept wandering." — recursive impossibility
- "I have an existential map. It has 'You are here' written all over it." — existence as bad cartography

### Technique demonstrations
- "There's a fine line between fishing and standing on the shore like an idiot." — reframe
- "I almost had a psychic girlfriend, but she left me before we met." — logic taken to conclusion
- "If at first you don't succeed, then skydiving isn't for you." — subverted advice
- "I busted a mirror and got seven years bad luck, but my lawyer thinks he can get me five." — register collision
