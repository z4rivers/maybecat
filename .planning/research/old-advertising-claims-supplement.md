**Vita Radium Suppositories (ca. 1930, Home Products Company, Denver)**
> "Weak Discouraged Men! Now Bubble Over with Joyous Vitality Through the Use of Glands and Radium."

> "If YOU are showing signs of 'slowing up' in your actions and duties, perhaps long before you should — if you have begun to lose your charm, your personality, your normal manly vigor — certainly you want to stage a 'comeback.'"

> "The man who has lost these precious attributes of youth knows how to appreciate their value. He realizes that happiness depends on his ability to perform the duties of a REAL MAN."

> "Sweet, glorious pleasures of life. Nature intended that you should enjoy them."

> "Now is the time to act! Today! RIGHT NOW! Tomorrow may never come."

> "Properly functioning glands make themselves known in a quick, brisk step, mental alertness and the ability to live and love in the fullest sense of the word."

> "Contains a result-producing amount of highly refined soluble RADIUM, carried in a cocoa butter base... absorbed thru the walls of the lower colon, enters the blood stream and is carried to all parts of the body — to the weakened organs that need its vitalizing aid."

> "Guaranteed entirely harmless."

> "Recommended for sexually weak men."

**Radithor (Bailey Radium Laboratories, 1920s)**
> "CERTIFIED Radioactive Water. Contains Radium and Mesothorium in Triple Distilled Water."

> "A Cure for the Living Dead."

> "Perpetual Sunshine."

> "Just a tiny bottle of apparently lifeless, colorless and tasteless water is all that the eye can see, or the tongue can detect, yet in this bottle there reposes the greatest therapeutic force known to mankind — radioactivity."

Bailey offered a $1,000 bounty to anyone who could prove a bottle contained less than one microcurie of radium. (The product was genuinely radioactive. That was the problem.)

**The Radiendocrinator (William Bailey, ca. 1924-1929)**
A gold case containing 250 millicuries of radium, designed to be "worn like an athletic supporter, directly under the scrotum." Claimed to treat diabetes, kidney disorders, prostate issues, arthritis, chronic headaches, and obesity "by ionizing the glands and promoting rejuvenation." Price: $150 (about $2,700 today).

**Revigator Water Crock (1920s-1930s)**
> "The millions of tiny rays that are continuously given off by this ore penetrate the water and form this great HEALTH ELEMENT — RADIO-ACTIVITY. All the next day the family is provided with two gallons of real, healthful radioactive water... nature's way to health."

> "A perpetual health spring in the home."

The name emphasized "VIG" — for vigor. Instructions recommended 6+ glasses daily of radon-infused water.

**Doramad Radioactive Toothpaste (Germany, 1920s-1940s)**
> "Its radioactive radiation increases the defenses of teeth and gums. The cells are loaded with new life energy, the bacteria are hindered in their destroying effect."

Original German: "Die Zellen werden mit neuer Lebensenergie geladen, die Bakterien in ihrer zerstörenden Wirksamkeit gehemmt."

**Tho-Radia Beauty Cream (France, 1933)**
> "Science Has Created Tho-Radia in Order to Beautify Women."

Invoked the name "Dr. Alfred Curie" — no relation to Marie Curie — for legitimacy.

**Radium Schokolade (Burk & Braun, Germany, 1931-1936)**
> "Eat This and Feel Great."

> "Of the highest medical value in the preservation or recovery of Health and invaluable to sportsmen, invalids and children."

Radioactive chocolate. Marketed to children.

**General Radium Ad Copy (various, 1920s)**
> "Radiate Youth and Beauty."
> "Radium is Restoring Health to Thousands."
> "Remarkable New Radium Cream Liniment Drives Out Pain from Aching Joints and Muscles Instantly!"

**Radium Mountain Water Co. (Shawnee, Oklahoma) — matchbook ad:**
> "Pure Elixir of Life — Stomach Disorders, Sugar Diabetes, Kidney Trouble. Ask Your Grocer He Knows!"

### Cocaine Products

**Cocaine Toothache Drops (Lloyd Manufacturing Co., Albany, NY, 1885)**
> "COCAINE TOOTHACHE DROPS. Instantaneous Cure! PRICE 15 CENTS."

The ad depicted children playing happily in a yard. For sale by all druggists.

**Vin Mariani Cocaine Wine (1860s-1910s)**
> "VIN MARIANI. POPULAR FRENCH TONIC WINE. Fortifies and Refreshes Body & Brain. Restores Health and Vitality."

Medical journal ad (November 1, 1890):
> "'VIN MARIANI' nourishes, strengthens, sustains and refreshes; is very palatable, and may be borne by the most enfeebled Stomach; never produces constipation, but, on the contrary, aids digestion and assimilation, removing fatigue and improving the appetite."

Pope Leo XIII carried a personal hip flask of it "to fortify himself when prayer was insufficient." He awarded Angelo Mariani a Vatican gold medal. Three consecutive Popes endorsed cocaine wine.

**Coca-Cola (1886)**
> "The Temperance Drink: A Valuable Brain Tonic and Cure for All Nervous Affections."

Contained approximately 9 milligrams of cocaine per glass until 1903. "Brain tonic" was, briefly, accurate.

**National Lead Company / Dutch Boy**

> "Lead concealed in the walls and under the floors of many modern buildings helps to give the best sanitation."

Dutch Boy White-Lead Paint ads:
> "Its blended as carefully as a Doctor's Prescription."

**"The Dutch Boy's Lead Party" (1923) — A coloring book for children**
Published by the National Lead Company as "A Paint Book for Girls and Boys." The story featured children living with "Old Man Gloom" in a drab, unpainted home. Dutch Boy arrives carrying a can of paint. The children welcome him to make their "gloomy life a little happier." He teaches kids how to paint their room, furniture, toys, and walls — with lead paint. Inside: a coupon for a can of lead paint that read "Give coupon to Mom or Dad."

The company also produced lead-based watercolors in children's paint books. For children. To put in their mouths. While painting.

### Baby Products (Morphine Edition)

**Mrs. Winslow's Soothing Syrup (1849-1930s)**
> "Has been used for over FIFTY YEARS by MILLIONS of MOTHERS for their CHILDREN WHILE TEETHING, with PERFECT SUCCESS."

> "It SOOTHES the CHILD, SOFTENS the GUMS, ALLAYS all PAIN, CURES WIND COLIC, and is the best remedy for DIARRHOEA."

Contained 65 mg of morphine per fluid ounce. One teaspoonful contained enough morphine to kill the average child. Nickname: "The Baby Killer." Many babies who took it went to sleep and never woke up.

### Feminine Hygiene (Floor Cleaner for Your Vagina)

**Lysol Douche Ads (1920s-1960s)**

Ad #1:
> "A man marries a woman because he loves her. So instead of blaming him if married love begins to cool, she should question herself. Is she truly trying to keep her husband and herself eager, happy married lovers? One most effective way to safeguard her dainty feminine allure is by practicing complete feminine hygiene as provided by vaginal douches with a scientifically correct preparation like 'Lysol.'"

Ad #2:
> "Sue was furious at Tom for the way he'd been treating her. But she was really to blame! She should have known better, for she was no stranger to feminine hygiene. It was just that she had been neglectful!"

"Feminine hygiene" was code for birth control (which couldn't be advertised directly). "Germs" meant sperm. Lysol contained cresol, which caused inflammation, burning, and death in some cases. It was also wildly ineffective as contraception. It was the best-selling method of contraception during the Great Depression.

### Pharmaceuticals Targeting Women

**Valium (1960s-1970s)**
> "For the Anxiety That Comes from Not Being Able to Cope."

One ad introduced "Jan, single and psychoneurotic, 35" with 15 years of failed relationships. Valium would make her "trim and smartly dressed" again — her students would "do a double-take."

Another ad showed Valium was for "prisoners of the society of stress" — illustrated by a woman shopping.

**Dexamyl — "Purple Hearts" (1950s-1960s)**
Amphetamine + barbiturate combo pill. Marketed as an anorectic, anxiolytic, AND antidepressant. For "tired housewives." The amphetamine lifts mood and suppresses appetite. The barbiturate prevents agitation. Speed and sedation in one pill, prescribed to millions of women for being alive in the 1950s.

**Thorazine (1950s-1960s)**
> "Tyrant in the house? Thorazine can help control the agitated, belligerent senile."

Also marketed for children's "hyperactivity," hostility, sleep problems, and vomiting. A major antipsychotic used to chemically manage inconvenient people into compliance.

### Asbestos

**"Pure White Fireproof Snow" (1930s-1950s)**
Asbestos fake snow for Christmas trees. "Pure White" and "Fire Proof."

**Johns-Manville Asbestos (1920s-1930s)**
> "Re-Roof for the Last Time!"
> "Double Protection — Safe and Secure!"

Women and children featured in advertisements to reinforce that asbestos made households healthier and safer. One ad promoted asbestos as "A clean and safe place for babies to crawl & play!"

## PART 4: TESTIMONIAL LANGUAGE — The Satisfied Customer Voice

The specific VOICE of the person telling you to buy the thing that's killing them.

### Patent Medicine Testimonial Structures
These appeared on trade cards, almanacs, and in newspaper ads. Common patterns:

- "I suffered for [length of time] with [condition]. Since using [product], I am completely cured."
- "My doctor told me there was no hope. Then I tried [product]."
- "I feel like a new man/woman since I started using [product]."
- Letters from "a sailor before the mast" or "a woman living in Philadelphia" — anonymous enough to be unverifiable, specific enough to feel real.
- The ASPCA president endorsing Sapanule: "commends it to all who need Relief from Suffering."
- Testimonials from Reverends, Doctors, and "Members of the General Public" — the holy trinity of trust.

### The Vin Mariani Celebrity Testimonial Machine
Angelo Mariani collected over 4,000 endorsements for his cocaine wine:
- Pope Leo XIII: gold medal, personal hip flask
- Pope Pius X: endorsement
- Pope Benedict XV: endorsement
- Thomas Edison: endorsement
- Ulysses S. Grant: endorsement
- Queen Victoria: endorsement
- Three Popes, two heads of state, and the inventor of the phonograph — all publicly endorsing cocaine wine.

### The Hadacol Testimonial
When asked on television whether Hadacol was good for anything, creator Dudley LeBlanc replied: "It was good for five and a half million dollars for me last year."

### The Wall Street Journal's Accidental Testimonial
On the death of Eben Byers (who drank 1,400 bottles of Radithor):
> "The Radium Water Worked Fine Until His Jaw Came Off."

### The Fleischmann's Yeast Method
Invented the disease "intestinal fatigue," blamed modern civilization for causing it, then sold the cure — raw yeast dissolved in water. Sales tripled. Consumer Reports disputed the claims. Nobody cared.

### The Listerine Method
Resurrected the obscure medical term "halitosis," turned bad breath into a disease, then sold the cure. Revenue went from $115,000 to $8 million in seven years. The campaign was called "whisper copy" — mimicking advice-column confessionals.

## PART 5: THE CONFIDENCE DISPROPORTION — The Comedy Engine

The MORE dangerous the product, the MORE absolute the claim. This is the core comedic principle.

### Danger Level vs. Confidence Level

| Product | What It Actually Does | What The Ad Says |
|---------|----------------------|------------------|
| Radium suppositories | Irradiates your colon | "Guaranteed entirely harmless" |
| Morphine baby syrup | Kills infants | "PERFECT SUCCESS" |
| Asbestos snow | Causes mesothelioma | "Pure White" |
| Lead paint | Poisons children | "Helps Guard Your Health" |
| DDT in nurseries | Carcinogen | "Helps make healthier homes" |
| Lysol douche | Chemical burns | "Scientifically correct" |
| Loaded revolver + child | Self-explanatory | "Papa Says It Won't Hurt Us" |
| Cocaine for toothaches | Addiction | "Instantaneous Cure!" |
| Radioactive toothpaste | Irradiates your mouth | "Cells loaded with new life energy" |
| Heroin for children | Addiction, death | "The Sedative for Coughs" |
| Cigarette with asbestos filter | Cancer + cancer | "So safe, so effective" |
| Floor cleaner as birth control | Burns, infection | "Dainty feminine allure" |

### The Pattern
The language of ABSOLUTE CERTAINTY only appears when the product is ABSOLUTELY LETHAL:
- "Not One Single Case" (of throat irritation from inhaling burning plant matter)
- "Accidental Discharge Impossible" (child holding loaded gun)
- "PERFECT SUCCESS" (morphine syrup for babies)
- "Guaranteed entirely harmless" (radium suppositories)
- "Certified Radioactive Water" (as if certification makes radiation safe)
- "Sanitized Tape Worms" (as if hygiene is the concern with parasites)

Nobody says "guaranteed harmless" about actual harmless things. You don't see "guaranteed harmless" on a pillow. The word "harmless" only appears when the product might kill you. The word "safe" only appears on ads for things that aren't.

## PART 6: ADDITIONAL PRODUCT CATEGORIES — Deep Cuts

### Amphetamines for Housewives (1950s-1960s)
- Smith, Kline & French marketed Benzedrine as a diet aid and mood improver
- AMA approved amphetamine advertising for weight loss in 1948
- Sales climbed to $7.3 million by 1949
- By 1970, 5% of Americans (9.7 million people) used prescription amphetamines
- "Rainbow diet pills" — multicolored speed sold to suburban women
- Dexamyl combined amphetamine + barbiturate — speed and sedation in one pill
- Andy Warhol got his Obetrol prescription in 1963; it became his drug of choice
- The Rolling Stones wrote "Mother's Little Helper" about this phenomenon

### Products That Literally Killed Their Users
1. **Radithor** — Eben Byers drank 1,400 bottles. His jaw disintegrated. His skull developed holes. He died.
2. **Mrs. Winslow's Soothing Syrup** — Morphine for babies. Mass infant deaths. Nicknamed "The Baby Killer."
3. **Radium Girls** — Factory workers told to lick radium paint brushes. Jaws rotted. Bones crumbled. Some glowed in the dark before death.
4. **Kent Micronite Filters** — The filter designed to make smoking "safer" was 15-25% crocidolite asbestos. Adding cancer to cancer.
5. **Lysol Douche** — Cresol caused inflammation, chemical burns, and death.
6. **Thalidomide** — Marketed as safe for pregnant women's morning sickness. Caused severe birth defects in over 10,000 children.

### The Tapeworm Diet — Authenticity Questions
The famous "Eat! Eat! Eat!" tapeworm ad may be a modern fabrication — parts of its design appear lifted from 1920s Neutroids ads. No tapeworm diet products appear in official FDA Notices of Judgment or the AMA's pamphlet on obesity cures. The ad may be fake, but the CONCEPT was real, and the advertising LANGUAGE is genuine regardless of provenance. The copy structure is what matters:
> "Eat! Eat! Eat! And Always Stay Thin! No diet, no baths, no exercise, no danger. FAT — the enemy that is shortening your life — BANISHED! How? With sanitized tapeworms. Jar packed."

### Shoe-Fitting Fluoroscopes — X-Raying Children for Fun
> "It's Dangerous to Have Your Child's Shoes Fitted Any Other Way."

10,000 X-ray machines in American shoe stores. Untrained sales clerks operating them. Children visited repeatedly for fun. The ad claimed NOT using radiation on your child's feet was the dangerous choice.
