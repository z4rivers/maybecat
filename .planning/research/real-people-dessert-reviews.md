# Real People's Dessert Review Language — Deep Research

Compiled from Yelp, Reddit, Amazon, Google reviews, Twitter, and Stanford linguistics research on 900,000 Yelp reviews. NOT food writers. Real humans struggling to describe what their mouth did.

- "I audibly moaned seeing this. My lord." — Reddit user on a perfectly layered croissant
- "OMG I almost came eating them" — GirlsAskGuys user on dry-rubbed ribs
- A reviewer at Firenze pizzeria described literally moaning while eating a Margherita pizza, praising "the golden, crisp crust, the messy and delicious cheese"

- Chocolate protein powder described as making someone's "eyes roll back and my loins tingle"

**The body-shutting-down category:**
- "Holy s*it, this is almost embarrassing" — a reviewer's first reaction to a Big Chocolate Cake, noting they "kept looking at each other, smiling and laughing while eating bite after bite"
- "Jaw-droppingly good sexy food" — real Yelp review (from Dan Jurafsky's Stanford study)
- "Orgasmic" pastry — actual Yelp descriptor found repeatedly in expensive restaurant reviews

**The drug metaphor category (Stanford linguistics research):**
- "These cupcakes, they're like crack."
- "The apple tarty ice cream pastry caramely thing was just orgasmic."
- "Succulent pork belly paired with seductively seared foie gras."
- Words that appear with increasing frequency as restaurant price rises: erotic, naughty, pornographic, seductive, sinful, voluptuous

## 5. THE TEXTURE WRESTLERS (People struggling to describe mouth-feel)

- Chocolate Peanut Butter Torte: "decadent, not overly sweet, creamy, crunchy" — listing sensations as they hit
- Fried Milk at Uchiko: "pillows that are crunchy on the outside and have a warm custard-like milk inside, and thick chocolate crisps that make a satisfying crunch"
- Raspberry beignets: "incredibly light and fluffy with the right amount of raspberry filling" with "the right amount of sugar on top so that your fingers weren't caked with powdered sugar." The experience described as "full" — "really well rounded and not thin in flavor."
- Negative Yelp: "The lemon flavoring is disgustingly artificial. I would not be shocked if this pathetic dessert was made of cleaning supplies: Lemon-scented Clorox wipes posing as icing and an old Mr. Clean Magic Eraser in place of pastry crust."

**The linguist's finding (Jurafsky, Stanford):**
Americans lean toward textural words for desserts over appearance, smell, taste, or sound. Words people reach for: moist, dense, creamy, sticky, gooey, oozing, dripping. Texture words like "sticky, silky, gooey" are associated with both dessert and sex in the human brain.

## 9. KEY LINGUISTIC PATTERNS (from Jurafsky's 900,000 Yelp reviews)

- **Sex metaphors for expensive food** — the more you pay, the more erotic the language
- **Drug metaphors for cheap food** — "addicted," "crack," "I need a fix" — absolves guilt
- **Women use drug metaphors more than men** — societal food guilt as "it made me eat it"
- **One-star reviews are almost never about the food** — they're about feeling disrespected
- **"Orgasmic" appears with measurable statistical frequency**, increasing with price point
- **Texture words dominate dessert descriptions** — how it FELT in their mouth before anything else

## 10. THE TEN LINGUISTIC MOVES OF NON-WRITERS DESCRIBING INCREDIBLE FOOD

1. **The body takeover** — "my eyes rolled back," "I moaned," "my knees went weak"
2. **The addiction frame** — "like crack," "I'm addicted," "I need this"
3. **The sex frame** — "orgasmic," "seductive," "sinful"
4. **The memory collapse** — "it tasted like my grandmother's kitchen"
5. **The inadequacy of language** — "I can't even describe," "words don't do it justice"
6. **The hyperbolic threat** — "I would die for this," "I would commit crimes"
7. **The ruination** — "this ruined all other [food] for me"
8. **The hug metaphor** — "like a warm hug," "like coming home"
9. **The texture spiral** — listing textures in sequence: "crispy then soft then gooey then..."
10. **The existential escalation** — food stops being food: "It didn't taste like food. It tasted like a decision."
