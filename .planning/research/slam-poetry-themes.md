# Slam Poetry / Spoken Word — Thematic Landscape (2024-2025)

What humans are actually bringing to microphones right now, organized by age group and territory.

---

## YOUNG POETS (Teens, Brave New Voices / Get Lit age)

The teens are not messing around. They're writing about survival — literal and emotional.

**What showed up at Get Lit's 13th Annual Classic Slam (LA, April 2024):**
- "It's Not That I Don't Remember My Childhood, I Just Grew Up Too Fast" — losing childhood, being parentified, growing up before you're ready
- "A Poem for Not All Men" — the fear women carry, the roles men play and downplay in a violent hypersexual society
- "Acceptable Alternatives to My Dead Name" — trans identity, the weight of a name
- "An Ode to Sweatpants Under Skirts" — the unwanted male gaze, being sexualized before you're ready
- "Name: Snow White, Occupation: Pretty" — appearance vs. reality, being reduced to how you look
- "The Math Lessons You'll Use for the Rest of Your Life" — body dysmorphia and eating disorders, counting calories dressed as math
- "Sea Levels" — critical, controlling partners (teens are already writing about toxic relationships)
- "Last Words Left on the White House Call-In Line" — Gaza, geopolitical conflict, feeling powerless about wars
- "Typhoons, Raindrops, Reflections" — racial identity and internalized racism
- "One Step Closer to Eating the Apple" — self-validation, the journey toward self-love
- "I Quit Dance" — leaving something you loved, choosing yourself over performance


**What Brave New Voices youth (13-19, nationwide) are bringing:**
- Mental health: not as abstract concept but as daily lived reality. Anxiety, depression, self-harm
- Bullying — still here, now also digital
- School shootings — the generation that does active shooter drills as routine. A 13-year-old won second place in a national writing contest for a poem about sitting in class wondering if "Shots fired!" would be her last words heard. Fourth graders in Philadelphia wrote poems from the prompt "Because there was a gun" — 24 of 30 students knew someone killed or wounded by a gun
- Climate anxiety — inheriting a planet they didn't break. Resentment at corporate greed and the Global North's overconsumption
- Pandemic aftermath — losing formative years, learning how to be around people again
- Identity: gender, sexuality, race, disability — intersecting, not siloed
- Immigration and being undocumented — especially among Latinx youth
- Adults failing them — problematic authority figures, parents who aren't safe

**What's SPECIFIC to this age group:**
- The loss of childhood itself — not nostalgia but THEFT. They didn't get to be kids
- First encounters with their own bodies being politicized or sexualized
- The rawness is different — less polished, more desperate, more "please hear me"
- Social media as the water they swim in, not something they adopted — comparison, performance, the curated self vs. the real self

## COLLEGE POETS (CUPSI age, 18-22ish)

CUPSI (College Unions Poetry Slam Invitational) is the big one. Teams of 4-5 from universities compete nationally.

**What college poets are wrestling with:**
- Identity at the intersection: being Black AND queer AND Southern AND first-gen AND disabled — not picking one lane
- Eating disorders and body image — Blythe Baird's "When The Fat Girl Gets Skinny" (over a million YouTube views) about going from being called fat to being congratulated for weight loss caused by an eating disorder. She published three collections between ages 17-21 covering mental illness, eating disorders, sexuality, feminism, trauma
- First-generation college student experience — code-switching between home and campus, feeling like an imposter in both spaces, parents who sacrificed everything for an education they can't understand
- Student debt as existential dread — 70% of graduating seniors say debt will influence what jobs they take. Almost a third plan gig work on top of full-time jobs. 80% of the class of 2024 report feeling burned out as undergrads
- Hustle culture and burnout — rejecting "girl boss" messaging, the glorification of exhaustion
- AI and what it means for their futures — Fiona Nugent's poem opens with "First they came for my em dashes" and addresses: AI using one bottle of water per 100-word email, entry-level jobs disappearing, the loneliness paradox where "the lonely build machines designed to teach us / How to be / Amongst each other"
- Dating apps and modern loneliness — 76% of Gen Z feel burned out on dating apps. The gamification of human connection
- Religious deconstruction — leaving the church, questioning faith they were raised in, the ex-vangelical movement
- Sexual assault and consent — still one of the most frequent topics at college slams
- Racism that isn't overt — "Jigga Monkey" at CUPSI was about insidious everyday racism, not just the obvious kind
- Gender identity and transition — dead names, pronouns, coming out, the nonbinary experience
- Neurodivergence — ADHD, autism, late diagnosis, "I'm not broken, I'm wired differently"

**What's SPECIFIC to this age group:**
- The "am I enough?" crisis — imposter syndrome meets identity formation
- Leaving home for the first time AND realizing home was complicated
- The pressure to have it figured out — career, identity, relationships, politics, all at once
- More theoretical/analytical framing than youth poets — they've read theory, they use frameworks, but the best ones keep it personal
- The gap between what college promised and what it delivers

## ADULT POETS (Open mic, NPS legacy, IWPS, 25+)

The National Poetry Slam ended in 2018, but the adult slam scene lives on through regional slams, open mics, the Nuyorican Poets Cafe Grand Slam, and platforms like Button Poetry.

**What adult slam poets are bringing:**

**Parenthood and family:**
- Clint Smith's "Above Ground" — becoming a father, seeing the world's joys and tragedies through new eyes, bear hugs and bath time alongside fear
- The mother-poet vs. father-poet split: mothers start attached and contemplate autonomy, fathers start separate and seek connection
- Raising kids in a world you're not sure about — climate, violence, politics
- Lennée Reid — Creole, queer, disabled single mom writing about "The Second Coming of Matriarchy"

**Race, systemic violence, and the body:**
- Ebony Stewart (2017 Woman of the World Poetry Slam Champion) — Black girlhood coming of age, trauma and recovery, mental illness, colorism, being treated as a sexual object. Collections: BloodFresh, Home.Girl.Hood., WASH
- Danez Smith — two-time Individual World Poetry Slam finalist, Don't Call Us Dead (National Book Award finalist). Racial discrimination, violence, sexuality, mental health
- Police brutality — not just the event but the AFTERMATH. The fear of becoming a hashtag
- Porsha Olayiwola — featured on Button Poetry's "Best Of" lists, addresses anti-Black violence

**Queer and trans life:**
- Andrea Gibson (1943-2025) — genderqueer poet, they/them pronouns, Colorado Poet Laureate 2023. Topics shifted from politics and social justice to depression, illness, mortality after cancer diagnosis in 2021. "Gender in the Key of Lyme Disease." Chronic illness as identity terrain
- Trans poets building entire presses — new words {press} specifically for transgender and gender-expansive voices
- Keath Silva — "Lady Death, and other poems venerating change" — transgender experience as a journey of letting go of falsity and embracing authenticity

**Love, forgiveness, and the interior life:**
- Rudy Francisco — one of the biggest names in spoken word right now. Race, class, gender, self-reflection, forgiveness. "Helium" tackles racial identity and mental health. "Excuse Me As I Kiss the Sky" (2023)
- Sarah Kay — still consistently featured, long-running exploration of wonder, connection, observation

**Housing, money, and class:**
- "The Rent EATS First" — Button Poetry publication title
- Gentrification poems from D.C., Seattle, Hamilton, Canada — "even the ghosts can no longer afford to rest in peace"
- Working-class poets writing about addiction in restaurant industries as a working condition
- The rise of "proletarian poetry" as a category

**Grief and loss:**
- National Poetry Slam Champion Regie Gibson — processing his father's death through spoken word
- Bianca Phipps — "Almosts" about losing a friend
- Ann E. Wallace — navigating long-haul COVID through poetry, Poet Laureate of Jersey City

**Addiction and recovery:**
- Button Poetry tags for addiction and alcoholism consistently populated
- Sobriety milestones as poetry
- The opioid/fentanyl crisis woven into class and geography

**Immigration:**
- "Here to Stay: Poetry and Prose from the Undocumented Diaspora" (2024) — 53 poets, DACA recipients, TPS holders, mixed-status families
- Undocupoets — nonprofit specifically supporting poets who are or were undocumented

**Disability and chronic illness:**
- Traci Neal — Black, autistic, late-diagnosed, using poetry to process not belonging
- Neurodivergent and neuroqueer poetics as its own emerging genre
- Chronic illness not as metaphor but as daily material reality

## TOPICS THAT CROSS ALL AGES

These show up at teen slams, college slams, and adult open mics alike:

1. **Mental health** — anxiety, depression, suicidal ideation, therapy, medication, the stigma around asking for help. The MOST universal slam topic right now. Not abstract — specific, named, embodied
2. **Identity** — who am I, really? Race, gender, sexuality, disability, class, geography, language — and how they collide
3. **Body** — eating disorders, body image, being sexualized, being invisible, the body as political territory
4. **Family trauma and intergenerational pain** — "I've inherited my mother's fear / And my father's bitterness." Parents who loved wrong. Parents who couldn't love at all. Parents who sacrificed everything. The immigrant family dynamic
5. **Love and relationships** — not just romance but friendship, self-love, toxic love, what it means to be seen
6. **Racism** — systemic, interpersonal, internalized. Police. The daily exhaustion
7. **Gender and sexuality** — coming out, transition, pronouns, the nonbinary experience, the queer body
8. **Gun violence** — from 4th graders to adults, at every age
9. **Social media and phones** — comparison, performance, addiction, the curated self, loneliness despite connection
10. **Grief** — death of loved ones, death of relationships, death of the person you used to be

## WHAT'S NEW (Wasn't in slam 5-10 years ago, or wasn't this loud)

**AI anxiety** — entry-level jobs disappearing, water usage, the loneliness paradox of building machines to teach us connection. Gen Z channeling this into poetry specifically. "First they came for my em dashes." This is BRAND NEW territory

**Climate grief as personal emotion, not activism** — moved from "save the planet" soapbox to "I'm mourning a future I won't have." Eco-anxiety as lived psychological condition, not political position. Youth poets at the Climate Museum's Apollo Theater slam: equal parts grief, anger, hope

**Post-pandemic aftermath** — not pandemic poems (those were 2020-2021) but the AFTER. Relearning how to be around people. The emotional distance that lingered even when society reopened. Long COVID as a physical and spiritual experience. The generation that lost formative years

**Religious deconstruction** — the ex-vangelical movement finding voice in poetry. Leaving the church. Questioning inherited faith. Not anti-religion but post-religion — "I used to believe and now I don't know what I believe"

**Neurodivergence as identity (not deficit)** — ADHD, autism, sensory processing, late diagnosis. "Wild-Wired: neurodivergent and neuroqueer poetics" as its own genre now. Not "overcoming" disability but claiming it

**Housing crisis as lived poetry** — gentrification, displacement, eviction, rent. Working-class poets naming the economic violence specifically. Moved from abstract "capitalism bad" to "I can't afford to live where I grew up"

**Trans and nonbinary experience normalized in slam** — not brave exception but regular presence. Entire presses dedicated to gender-expansive voices. Dead name poems, pronoun poems, but also just LIVING poems that happen to be from trans poets

**Hustle culture rejection** — the "soft living" movement, refusing to glorify burnout, naming the exhaustion. Specific to post-2020

**Palestine/Gaza** — not just political solidarity but deeply personal for Arab-American poets. Refaat Alareer wrote "If I must die" before being killed in an Israeli airstrike; it went massively viral. Mahmoud Darwish rediscovered — 18 million TikTok views. Youth poets writing "Last Words Left on the White House Call-In Line." This topic is EVERYWHERE in current slam

**Dating app burnout** — the gamification of human connection, swiping as dehumanization, the paradox of infinite choice and total loneliness. Specific to this generation's relationship with technology

## WHAT'S PERENNIAL (Always there, still alive, keeps evolving)

**Race** — always the backbone of American slam. But the conversation evolves: from civil rights to Black Lives Matter to anti-Blackness to colorism to intersectional analysis. Ebony Stewart, Danez Smith, Clint Smith, Porsha Olayiwola carrying it forward

**Gender and feminism** — from "women's issues" to intersectional feminism to gender abolition. The conversation about masculinity has shifted: Guante's "Ten Responses to the Phrase 'Man Up'" (millions of views) challenging toxic masculinity. 97% of people say masculinity is changing. Men's vulnerability as poetic territory

**Love** — the oldest poetry topic, still alive. But the texture has changed: love in the age of algorithms, love across identity lines, self-love as radical act, love in the context of trauma

**Family** — immigrant family dynamics, absent fathers, overbearing mothers, the parent you became vs. the parent you had. "Shrinking Women" by Lily Myers (3+ million views) about inheriting her mother's tendency to take up less space

**Death and grief** — universal, timeless, but now includes gun death, overdose death, COVID death, suicide. The ways we die have changed; the poems follow

**Class and poverty** — always present in slam, which was literally born in working-class Chicago bars as a way to reclaim poetry from elite institutions. Still the DNA

**The body** — its hunger, its shame, its pleasure, its politicization. Eating disorders, chronic illness, aging, beauty standards. The body as the first and last territory

**Addiction** — from crack to opioids to phones. The substance changes; the pattern stays. Recovery poems, sobriety poems, relapse poems

## THE EMOTIONAL LANDSCAPE (What feelings are people bringing)

Not just topics — the FEELINGS underneath:

- **Rage** — at systems, at people who should have protected them, at injustice. Controlled burn, not explosion
- **Grief** — for what was lost, for what never was, for what won't be. Not wallowing but NAMING
- **Fear** — of guns, of the future, of not being enough, of the planet dying
- **Defiance** — "I exist and you can't erase me." Trans poets, disabled poets, undocumented poets. Existence as resistance
- **Tenderness** — the soft underbelly. Love poems, forgiveness poems, poems to their younger selves. Often the most powerful pieces
- **Exhaustion** — burnout, compassion fatigue, being tired of fighting, being tired of explaining
- **Hope** — not naive hope but EARNED hope. The kind that costs something. The choice to keep going
- **Humor** — dark humor, self-deprecating humor, humor as survival mechanism. The funny poem that makes you cry
- **Loneliness** — the great epidemic. Connected to everything but belonging to nothing

## REGIONAL AND CULTURAL NOTES

- **The Twin Cities (Minneapolis)** — where Button Poetry is based, strong BIPOC and LGBTQ+ scene, fascism and authoritarianism as explicit topics given recent political climate
- **Los Angeles (Get Lit)** — huge youth scene, gentrification as lived experience, Latinx and immigrant voices prominent
- **New York (Nuyorican Poets Cafe)** — the origin, still active, Grand Slam 2024 at the Apollo Theater. $1,000 prize. The cafe that started it all in the 1970s
- **D.C.** — gentrification of "Chocolate City" as major theme, political proximity means more government/policy-focused work
- **The Caribbean** — First Citizens National Poetry Slam (12th edition in 2024), largest cash prize for spoken word in the world (TT$50,000)
- **UK** — Big Church Poetry Slam (Bible-inspired), less identity-politics focus, more spiritual/existential
- **Australia** — Real Talk program in western Sydney, multilingual and multicultural youth creating spoken word
