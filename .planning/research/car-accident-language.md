# Car Accident Language Research
*Compiled: 2026-02-15*

A comprehensive collection of the actual language, phrases, and vocabulary used to describe car accidents across different contexts: insurance claims, police reports, personal stories, and mechanic/body shop descriptions.

---

## INSURANCE CLAIM LANGUAGE

### Formal Descriptive Phrases

**Standard Timeline Structure:**
- "On [date and time of accident], I was driving my [make and model of the car] on [street and direction of travel]"
- "Before the accident:" describing actions leading up to crash
- "During the accident:" explaining the moment of impact
- "After the accident:" what happened immediately after

**Impact and Collision:**
- "The other vehicle ran a red light and hit the driver's side of my car"
- "The impact caused damage to the front left side of my vehicle"
- "At the intersection of [cross street]"
- "Rear-ended at a stoplight"
- "I was in a head-on collision"
- "The other vehicle swerved into my lane hitting me"
- "There was an animal in the opposing lane of traffic"
- "My car was totaled"

**Location and Direction:**
- "The other vehicle" / "the other party" / "the other driver"
- "At-fault driver"
- "Third-party claim"
- "Traveling in [direction]"
- "Which road you were driving on and which direction you were traveling"
- "The opposing lane"

### Funny/Absurd Insurance Claim Quotes (ACTUAL)

**The Invisible/Nowhere Category:**
- "An invisible car came out of nowhere, struck my car, and vanished"
- "Came out of nowhere"
- "I don't know who was to blame for the accident; I wasn't looking"
- "I left my car unattended for a minute and, whether by accident or design, it ran away"

**Anatomical Impossibilities:**
- "I had one eye on the parked car, another on an approaching truck, and another on the woman behind" (three eyes!)
- "I was driving along when I saw two kangaroos copulating in the middle of the road causing me to ejaculate through the sun roof" (Australian claim)

**Food & Objects:**
- "A potato being lodged behind the brake pedal"
- "The claimant's car was damaged by a frozen kebab that was tossed out of another car"
- "In an attempt to kill a fly I drove into a telephone pole"

**Animal Encounters:**

- **Q:** "What warning was given by the other party?" **A:** "Moo"
- "I was driving home and as I approached the junction a hedge sprang up, obscuring my vision so I could not see the other vehicle. I started to turn and it was at this point I noticed a camel and an elephant tethered at the verge"

**Family & Distractions:**
- "I pulled away from the side of the road, glanced at my mother-in-law, and headed over the embankment"
- "Going to work at 7am this morning I drove out of my drive straight into a bus. The driver and passengers then left immediately for a vacation with injuries"

**Parking & Maneuvering:**
- "I was driving along the motorway when the police pulled me over onto the hard shoulder. Unfortunately I was in the middle lane and there was another car in the way"
- "A hedge sprang up, obscuring my vision"

## POLICE REPORT LANGUAGE

### Official Terminology

**Vehicle Designation:**
- "Unit 1" / "Vehicle 1" (often the striking vehicle or perceived cause)
- "Unit 2" / "Vehicle 2"
- "Vehicle 1 struck Vehicle 2"
- NOTE: These designations don't legally determine fault, just organize the report

**Report Structure:**
- "Investigating officers will often designate the vehicle they think caused the accident as Unit 1, but this is not a legal determination of fault"
- "Descriptive narrative within the accident report"
- "The first page contains the basics: when the collision occurred, where it happened, where the injured were sent, and which police department responded"

**Accident Classification:**
- "Pedestrian crash"
- "Collision within the road proper"
- "PDOF" (Principal Direction of Force) - e.g., "10 or 11 o'clock for driver-side impacts, and 2 o'clock for passenger side impacts"

**Technical Codes:**
- Ten-codes (numerical communication via CB radio)
- ETA (Estimated Time of Arrival)
- LEO (Law Enforcement Officer)

**Diagrams:**
- "Showing the positions and directions of vehicles relative to key features of the accident scene such as intersections and fixed objects"

## COLLISION TYPES & MECHANICS

### Types of Impacts

**Rear-End:**
- "Rear-ended at a stoplight"
- "Rear-ended while stopped"
- "Couldn't stop in time"
- "Failed to stop in time"
- "Crashing into the rear of the lead vehicle"
- "Chain reaction of rear-end collisions"

**Side-Impact:**
- "T-boned" / "T-bone accident" / "T-bone collision"
- "Sideswiped"
- "Clipped"
- "Side collision"
- "Impact on the driver's side"
- "Impact on the passenger side"
- "Struck on the driver's side" / "struck on the passenger side"
- "Hit the side of another vehicle perpendicularly, forming a 'T' shape"
- "When your car is t-boned and you're seated on the impact side, there's only a door and a window between you and the striking vehicle"

**Head-On:**
- "Head-on collision"
- "Frontal impact"
- "Front ends of two vehicles hit each other when travelling in opposite directions"
- "The force of the impact is doubled due to the travelling speed of each vehicle"
- "Combined speeds of the vehicles create a high-power impact"

**Minor Collisions:**
- "Fender bender"
- "Minor collision"
- "Bumper damage"
- "Low-speed crash"
- "Parking lot bump"
- "Minor cosmetic or light structural damage"
- "Dents"

**Rollovers:**
- "Rolled over"
- "Flipped"
- "Rolled three times"
- "Multiple flips"
- "The vehicle tips over onto its side or roof"
- "Flipped on its side"
- "Tripped rollover" (caused by external object like curb)
- "Untripped rollover" (steering input, speed, friction)
- "The faster and more times a vehicle flips, the more severe the injuries"

## BODY SHOP & MECHANIC LANGUAGE

### Damage Assessment Terms

**Structural Damage:**
- "Frame damage"
- "Crumple zone"
- "Crumple zones are parts of the frame which crumple during a collision in order to absorb some of the impact"
- "Naturally dented parts of the frame, which are specially designed to bend and fold"
- "Strategically located on the car's front fascia, the quarter panel, and the rear bumper"
- "Minor crumple zone damage"
- "Structural repair"
- "Frame straightening"

**Total Loss Terminology:**
- "Total loss"
- "Totaled"
- "Write-off"
- "Written off"
- "Salvage"
- "Salvage value"
- "It would cost more to return to marketable condition than the market value"
- "About one in seven car accident claims results in a 'total'"
- "Actual cash value" (ACV)
- "Cost of repairs plus the value of remaining parts exceeds the value of the car"

**Estimate Terminology:**
- "Line item" (numerical list describing every damaged component)
- "FEA" (front end alignment)
- "FWA" (front wheel alignment)
- "4WA" (alignment for all four wheels)
- "LKQ" (Like-Kind-Quality - used parts from salvaged vehicles)
- "OEM" (Original Equipment Manufacturer parts)
- "A/M" (aftermarket parts)
- "B%" (betterment - percentage after deducting for wear and tear/depreciation)

**Damage Descriptions:**
- "Bodywork damage"
- "External panels and structures"
- "Scratches, dents, rust, and paint damage"
- "Structural distortion"
- "Additional damages hiding beneath the surface"
- "Front left side"
- "Lower side interior (door)"
- "Hood buckled upward"
- "Headlamp shattered"
- "Bumper hung like a broken jaw"
- "Bumpers, fenders, taillights, or grilles"

**Assessment Process:**
- "Laser measuring"
- "Manufacturer-approved replacement parts"
- "Minor deformation"
