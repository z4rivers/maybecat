# Hardcore Punk Raw Material — "There Is Real Underneath the Chaos"

Research compiled 2026-02-13. Actual quotes from verified publications, books, documentaries, and lyrics.

**Creative direction:** The surprising wisdom hiding under punk aggression. Not just angry — genuinely insightful. Lines that are poetic, surprising, and TRUE.

**Why this is catlike:** Punk cats don't conform, don't need your approval, see through bullshit, are fiercely independent, have integrity on their own terms. The DIY ethos = cat self-sufficiency. "I don't need to be loud to be right" — the quiet punk truth that IS cat energy.

## IAN MacKAYE (Minor Threat / Fugazi / Dischord Records)

### Interview Quotes

1. **"If you want to rebel against society, don't dull the blade."**
   — WAMU Bandwidth interview. Full context: "The only option made readily available was self-destruction. So in schools or any arena, the kids who were the rebellious ones were the ones who were hurting themselves. And that just seemed counterproductive to me. If you wanted to rebel against society, don't dull the blade."
   **[POETIC / SURPRISING]** — This flips rebellion completely. Sobriety as the sharpest form of defiance.

2. **"The world is filled with trash and I'm interested in not adding to that pile."**
   — Loud and Quiet interview (2016). On why he doesn't release music unless it matters.
   **[SURPRISING]** — The punk who built a label talking about RESTRAINT.

3. **"Music is becoming content... but music is deep. That's not music; that's just the marketplace, that's just business. Music was here long before the music industry."**
   — Loud and Quiet interview (2016).

4. **"My definition of punk is the free space. It's an area in which new ideas can be presented without having to go through the filtration or perversion of profiteering."**
   — Louder Sound interview.
   **[POETIC]** — "Free space" is a beautiful definition.

5. **"The moment is the point — that's the success. Make music that you love, with the people you love. That's what I would do."**
   — Illinois State University talk (November 2025).

6. **"Do the thing you love that you think is important."**
   — Illinois State University talk (November 2025).

7. **"I supplanted the idea of 'scene' with tribe early on... my interest in punk rock was that I wanted to create a family."**
   — Huck Magazine (Issue 49, 2015).
   **[SURPRISING]** — The godfather of hardcore wanted a FAMILY.

8. **"Let's create a bloc, let's be a part of something. Let's stay in touch, and visit each other and collect. That's what punk was."**
   — Huck Magazine.

9. **"We don't use contracts, lawyers, any of those kinds of things. We are partners — they make the music, and we make the records."**
   — SPIN interview (2003), on Dischord Records.

10. **"We can either split the money up, or we can make a yearbook... ultimately, when you look back at it, it's the documentation."**
    — On Dischord's purpose. SPIN interview (2003).
    **[POETIC]** — A punk label as a yearbook. A record of being alive.

11. **"What I loved about punk rock was that the audience was just there, almost by default — there was no profit; profit wasn't part of the equation."**
    — Louder Sound interview.

12. **"The music industry works, they seek the fertile ground and then they rape the ground and then they move on. They don't take care of the soil at all, they don't care: it's just about maximising profits, get in, get out."**
    — Louder Sound interview.

13. **"For me, music is sacred. I'm not talking about rock shows. I'm talking about the real potential of people gathering and making a joyful noise and reacting to that noise."**
    — Various interviews. Source: Far Out Magazine / Louder Sound.

14. **"Personal purifying is the beginning of everything. Once you get your own shit together... it makes life for you and the people around you so much more agreeable."**
    — Quoted in Michael Azerrad's *Our Band Could Be Your Life* (2001).

### Lyrics

15. **"You are not what you own."**
    — Fugazi, "Merchandise," *Repeater* (1990). Dischord Records.
    **[POETIC / SURPRISING]** — Five words. Said everything.

16. **"I don't drink / I don't smoke / I don't fuck / At least I can fucking think."**
    — Minor Threat, "Out of Step," *Out of Step* (1983). Dischord Records.

### On Straight Edge Philosophy

17. **"Straight Edge is an anti-obsession pro-positive song and it's not telling people what to do, it's not a set of rules."**
    — Brand New Age zine interview.
    **[SURPRISING]** — The man who accidentally started a movement says it was never meant to be one.

18. **"The point of the song was pretty obvious to me: I don't do all these things, but at least I can think."**
    — On "Out of Step." Various interviews.

## HENRY ROLLINS (Black Flag / Spoken Word / Writer)

### From *Get in the Van* (book, 1994; Grammy-winning spoken word, 1995)

19. **"It would be nice to be touched by hands that didn't want to kill or congratulate me. Sometimes the hands that want to kill me are mine."**
    — *Get in the Van: On the Road with Black Flag* (2.13.61 Publications, 1994).
    **[POETIC / SURPRISING]** — The most violent man in punk writing about tenderness.

20. **"There are a few things that are mine... Like today, sitting in this diner. This time is mine. These thoughts are mine. Memories are mine. I don't own much. It's nice to know that there are some things that no one will be able to take from me."**
    — *Get in the Van*.
    **[POETIC]** — A man who owns nothing discovering what's actually his.

21. **"Discipline is money in the bank. A real friend, true strength."**
    — *Get in the Van*.

22. **"I learned that you can get away with a lot of shit if you just do it like it's all you knew how to do."**
    — *Get in the Van*.
    **[SURPRISING]** — Confidence as the only credential.

### From *Solipsist* (book, 1998)

23. **"I love you more than I hate my loneliness and pain."**
    — *Solipsist* (2.13.61 Publications, 1998).
    **[POETIC / SURPRISING]** — Henry Rollins. Wrote THAT.

24. **"The moon will never lie to anyone. Be like the moon. No one hates the moon or wants to kill it... The moon never shoves clouds out of its way so it can be seen. The moon needs not fame or money to be powerful. The moon never asks you to go to war to defend it."**
    — *Solipsist*.
    **[POETIC / SURPRISING]** — The screamer from Black Flag wrote a love letter to the moon.

25. **"I got my heart broken. My spirit got shattered and mutilated. I will not be coming back from this. I don't want to."**
    — *Solipsist*.

### From "The Iron and the Soul" (essay, published in Details magazine, 1994)

26. **"The Iron never lies to you. You can walk outside and listen to all kinds of talk, get told that you're a god or a total bastard. The Iron will always kick you the real deal."**
    — "Iron and the Soul," originally published in Details magazine (1994).
    **[POETIC / SURPRISING]** — The purest statement of honesty in all of punk. A barbell as truth-teller.

27. **"It never freaks out on me, never runs. Friends may come and go. But two hundred pounds is always two hundred pounds."**
    — "Iron and the Soul."
    **[POETIC]** — The most reliable relationship in his life was with gravity.

28. **"Scar tissue is stronger than regular tissue. Realize the strength, move on."**
    — "Iron and the Soul."

### Interview Quotes

29. **"Loneliness adds beauty to life. It puts a special burn on sunsets and makes night air smell better."**
    — Various interviews / LA Weekly column.
    **[POETIC / SURPRISING]** — This is Rilke-level. From the guy who screamed "Rise Above."

30. **"Knowledge without mileage equals bullshit."**
    — Various interviews, widely attributed. Appears in *The Portable Henry Rollins* (Villard, 1997).

31. **"Cynicism is only intellectual sloth."**
    — Various interviews.
    **[SURPRISING]** — A punk icon calling cynicism LAZY.

32. **"Everything I do, writing, touring, travelling, it all comes from the punk and hardcore attitude, from that expression — from being open to try things but relying on yourself, taking what you have into the battle and making of it what you will."**
    — Kerrang! interview.

33. **"Strength is kindness and sensitivity. Strength is understanding that your power is both physical and emotional."**
    — Various interviews.
    **[SURPRISING]** — The most muscular man in punk defining strength as KINDNESS.

34. **"You always know the mark of a coward. A coward hides behind freedom. A brave person stands in front of freedom and defends it for others."**
    — Various interviews / columns.

35. **"For me, it was a gladiator sport. The music is trying to kill me, and I'm trying to keep it from killing me."**
    — RetroFuturista interview.

36. **"Bebop jazz is every bit as punk rock as anything that Joe Strummer ever did; that Miles Davis was a total punk rock visionary."**
    — The Quietus interview.
    **[SURPRISING]** — Punk's poster boy calling Miles Davis punk rock.

## D. BOON & MIKE WATT (Minutemen)

### D. Boon

37. **"Punk is whatever we made it to be."**
    — Attributed to D. Boon, quoted extensively by Mike Watt in interviews including Weekly Alibi and The Creative Independent.
    **[POETIC]** — Seven words. The entire philosophy. "We made it. Not I — we."

38. **D. Boon "could use simple-ass words and get huge-ass meaning out of 'em... was like Whitman in that way."**
    — Mike Watt, in Michael Azerrad's *Our Band Could Be Your Life* (Little, Brown, 2001).
    **[SURPRISING]** — A punk compared to Walt Whitman. And it's accurate.

### Mike Watt

39. **"We jam econo."**
    — Minutemen ethos. Song title, album philosophy, documentary title. Widely attributed.
    **Full context from Azerrad's book:** "You could jam econo on your job, in your buying habits, in your whole way of living. You could take this particular approach to music and apply it to just about anything else you wanted to do."
    **[SURPRISING]** — A life philosophy disguised as a budget strategy.

40. **"Corporate rock was about living large; indie was about living realistically and being proud of it."**
    — Michael Azerrad, *Our Band Could Be Your Life*, describing the Minutemen's ethos.

41. **"You need bad things to make good things. It's like with farming — if you want to grow a good crop, you need a lot of manure."**
    — Mike Watt, quoted in *Our Band Could Be Your Life*.
    **[POETIC]** — Working-class wisdom that's actually profound.

42. **"The knowing is in the doing. You find out where the wall is by pushing against it."**
    — Mike Watt, Heavy Blog Is Heavy interview (2017) and The Creative Independent (2019).
    **[POETIC / SURPRISING]** — Pure pragmatic punk philosophy. Stop thinking. Push.

43. **"I'm D. Boon's bass player."**
    — Mike Watt, Believer Magazine interview. Said decades after D. Boon's death.
    **[POETIC / SURPRISING]** — Loyalty stated in present tense for someone who died in 1985.

44. **"Punk is kind of like a big hay wagon. If you've got something to bring, come on."**
    — Mike Watt, quoting Joey Ramone. Believer Magazine interview / The Creative Independent.
    **[SURPRISING]** — Punk as open invitation, not gatekeeping.

45. **"All of us in the Minutemen were from working class families. But we found ways to still do what we could, and not let material shortcomings totally snuff out our creative fires."**
    — Mike Watt, The Beijinger interview (2017).

46. **"Lowering your sights was raising your sights."**
    — Describing the Minutemen philosophy. Michael Azerrad, *Our Band Could Be Your Life*.
    **[POETIC / SURPRISING]** — The paradox at the heart of DIY.

## KATHLEEN HANNA (Bikini Kill / Le Tigre / Riot Grrrl)

47. **"I would much rather be the obnoxious feminist girl than be complicit in my own dehumanization."**
    — Various interviews.
    **[SURPRISING]** — The math of self-respect, stated plainly.

48. **"Girls to the front."**
    — Stage direction at Bikini Kill shows. Context from NPR (2024): "We weren't the first girl punk band; there have always been feminist punks. But there was a time when there were only three girls in the room among all these guys, and so you needed to say, for their safety, if nothing else, 'Girls, come to the front.'"
    **[SURPRISING]** — Not a slogan. A safety instruction.

49. **"There is no HR in punk rock."**
    — CBC Arts interview (2024), on sexism in the punk scene.
    **[POETIC / SURPRISING]** — The funniest and saddest observation about punk's blind spot.

50. **"I'm not going to sit around and be peace and love with somebody's boot on my neck."**
    — Various interviews.

51. **"I thought of myself as a feminist performance artist who was in a punk band."**
    — NPR interview (2024), on Bikini Kill.

52. **"Well, this is the price of being female in this business."**
    — CBC Arts interview (2024). What she told herself when facing sexism on tour.
    **[SURPRISING]** — The resignation before the revolution.

### Riot Grrrl Manifesto (Bikini Kill Zine #2, 1991)

53. **"BECAUSE we must take over the means of production in order to create our own meanings."**
    — Riot Grrrl Manifesto, Bikini Kill Zine #2 (1991).
    **[POETIC]** — Marx meets punk meets feminism in one sentence.

54. **"BECAUSE we are angry at a society that tells us Girl = Dumb, Girl = Bad, Girl = Weak."**
    — Riot Grrrl Manifesto, Bikini Kill Zine #2 (1991).

55. **"BECAUSE we hate capitalism in all its forms and see our main goal as sharing information and staying alive, instead of making profits or being cool according to traditional standards."**
    — Riot Grrrl Manifesto, Bikini Kill Zine #2 (1991).
    **[SURPRISING]** — "Sharing information and staying alive" as the whole mission.

## BAD BRAINS (H.R.)

60. **"We got that PMA" — Positive Mental Attitude.**
    — Bad Brains, "Attitude," *Bad Brains* (ROIR cassette, 1982).
    Full context: The fast songs are not calls for violence; they're an expression of the human dichotomy and validation of the need to vent one's anger without causing others harm.
    **[SURPRISING]** — The fastest, hardest band in punk was preaching OPTIMISM.

61. **H.R. is "philosophically into a non-violent thing, and he's particular about language — avoiding phrases like 'punch that part in,' instead saying 'Don't say punch.'"**
    — Various interviews / profiles. Louder Sound.
    **[SURPRISING]** — The most intense performer in hardcore was a pacifist about VOCABULARY.

62. **"PMA was really the Great Spirit."**
    — Earl Hudson (Bad Brains drummer), explaining their philosophy. Iowa Public Radio (2025).

## GUY PICCIOTTO (Rites of Spring / Fugazi)

63. **"For me, getting on stage and performing has always been this intense carbonation — a sense of not being able to control the feeling."**
    — Various interviews. Creative Loafing.
    **[POETIC]** — "Intense carbonation." The man who invented emo describing it as fizzy.

64. **"When I was young, I was always over the top because I was so fucked up. Not 'fucked up' as in 'wasted' but more mentally 'fucked up.' So it came out of that."**
    — On the origins of emotional hardcore. Various interviews.
    **[SURPRISING]** — Emo wasn't a genre. It was a symptom.

65. **"I woke up this morning with a piece of past caught in my throat."**
    — Rites of Spring, "For Want Of," *Rites of Spring / End on End* (Dischord, 1985).
    **[POETIC / SURPRISING]** — This is poetry. Full stop. From a hardcore record.

## DENNIS LYXZEN (Refused)

66. **"How can we expect anyone to listen if we are using the same old voice?"**
    — Refused, "New Noise," *The Shape of Punk to Come* (Burning Heart, 1998).
    **[POETIC / SURPRISING]** — The mission statement. Screamed.

67. **"For me punk came with an open world of possibilities. It liberated me from what people thought I would become."**
    — Loudwire interview (2019).

68. **"I'm an opinionated bastard but not rigid... an open and flexible mind."**
    — Various interviews. Antimatter Substack.
    **[SURPRISING]** — The manifesto guy admitting he's still figuring it out.

69. **"As far as the manifesto goes, it's kind of awesome and it's stupid at the same time... It's one of those things where it's just so pretentious and over the top that it's just funny."**
    — On *The Shape of Punk to Come* manifesto. Antimatter Substack interview.
    **[SURPRISING]** — Self-awareness from the guy who wrote a revolutionary treatise.

70. **"I really chafe at the idea that political conditions are good for art — or specifically punk music. I don't like that idea as if having high-quality consumables is a good trade-off for living under a fascist regime."**
    — Kerrang! interview.
    **[SURPRISING]** — A political punk rejecting the "good art needs suffering" myth.

## BLAKE SCHWARZENBACH (Jawbreaker)

71. **Schwarzenbach's lyrics "read like scuffed-up poetry, full of doubt, defiance, and those tiny moments that feel bigger than they should."**
    — Kerrang!, "Decoding Jawbreaker's Monumental 24 Hour Revenge Therapy" (2019).

72. **"I work furtively in the shadows, and try to get an honest take when no one's looking."**
    — Blake Schwarzenbach, quoted in Dan Ozzi's *Sellout* (Mariner Books, 2021). On recording *Dear You* while Rancid recorded confidently nearby.
    **[POETIC / SURPRISING]** — The anti-rockstar. Punk as humility.

73. **Jawbreaker's "poetic take on hardcore"** — Schwarzenbach's narrative form was in line with Ginsberg, Kerouac, and Salinger. He studied their works while working as a librarian.
    — Kerrang! / Wikipedia sourcing from multiple music publications.
    **[SURPRISING]** — The punk frontman was a librarian who read Kerouac on shift.

    — Kerrang! interview.
    **[SURPRISING]** — A punk musician calling punk music inadequate.

76. **"I see the band now as more of a conduit for marginalised voices rather than considering the band an activist project."**
    — New Noise Magazine interview.

## FROM *OUR BAND COULD BE YOUR LIFE* (Michael Azerrad, 2001)

82. **"The greatest thing about punk rock for me, as an outsider, was that the concept that you had to be allowed in was no longer valid. You could be operating in a vacuum and if you did something of worth, all these external conditions were immaterial."**
    — Steve Albini, quoted in *Our Band Could Be Your Life*.
    **[POETIC / SURPRISING]** — Permission was no longer required. That's the revolution.

83. **"The Replacements secretly believed in themselves and yet adopted a loser persona to insulate themselves against failure."**
    — Azerrad's description. *Our Band Could Be Your Life*.
    **[SURPRISING]** — Every imposter syndrome ever, described via a punk band.

84. **"We don't have to convince the world that we're suffering to convince them that we're artists."**
    — Attributed in discussion of Minutemen vs. Black Flag contrast. *Our Band Could Be Your Life*.
