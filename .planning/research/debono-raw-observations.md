# De Bono — Raw Observations (Unharvested)

Things NOT in HARVESTED.md. The observations themselves, stripped of technique framing.

---

The brain is like a landscape. Rainfall (new information) flows along existing drainage channels. The channels were carved by old water. New water just follows them. Takes deliberate effort to redirect the flow.

The patterns in the brain are asymmetric. You travel the main road easily but can't even SEE the side roads. You don't know you're missing them.

But if you somehow get onto the side road, the route back is completely obvious. That's what insight is — not discovery, just suddenly being able to see the road you were already on.

A joke and a creative breakthrough use the exact same brain mechanism. The storyteller walks you down the main track, the punchline drops you on the side track, you see the connection back to where you started — and that's why you laugh. Humor is creativity happening TO you. Lateral thinking is creativity you do ON PURPOSE.

"Humor is by far the most significant activity of the human brain." He said this completely seriously.

The brain, when presented with two equally valid explanations, will pick one and completely ignore the other. It cannot hold both. It doesn't balance them. It eliminates one.

New information gets pulled into the nearest existing pattern even when it doesn't quite fit. The patterns have large "catchment areas." This is why first impressions last and stereotypes stick — the brain is literally designed to assimilate incoming data into whatever pattern it already has.

"A memory is what is left when something happens and does not completely unhappen."

"You do not go out into the street in your underwear, although usually you are wearing underwear." Not everything that is present needs to be visible. Not everything that's hidden is absent.

"Simplicity before understanding is simplistic; simplicity after understanding is simple." The same thing is either stupid or brilliant depending on which side of understanding you're on.

Precise concepts LOCK thinking. Blurry concepts UNLOCK it. "I need a match" stops you if there are no matches. "I need SOME WAY of lighting this fire" opens everything. The less specific the shape of the hole, the more things can fill it.

"A pre-concept is defined by its desired function before finding what fills it." You can hold the shape of a solution you don't have yet.

"In 80% of Socrates' dialogues there was no constructive outcome." Socrates just identified what was wrong. De Bono's point: Western culture inherited this and decided that finding fault IS thinking. It isn't.

"The system will always be defended by those countless people who have enough intellect to defend but not quite enough to innovate."

"Critical thinking does seem a superior sort of thinking because it seems as though the critic is actually going beyond the scope of what is being criticized." It FEELS smarter. It isn't.

"A person who knows all the answers, has an opinion on everything, has a certainty backed up by rational argument, has very little possibility of further progress."

"Being right all the time acquires huge importance in education, and there is this terror of being wrong." The terror is learned.

"To cultivate a pleasure in being wrong sounds perverse, yet losing an argument means escaping from an old idea." Being wrong is the only way out.

"Unfortunately, our existing traditional thinking habits insist that you must attack something and show it to be bad before you can suggest a change." You can only propose something new if you first prove the old thing is broken. But what if the old thing works fine and the new thing is just better?

"The need to be right all the time is the biggest bar to new ideas."

Rock logic is based on the word IS. Water logic is based on the word TO. "What IS this?" vs. "What does this LEAD TO?" The whole difference between stuck and moving is which word you're using.

"A ball on a slope rolls 'to' the bottom. A river flows 'to' the sea." Water logic asks what happens NEXT. Rock logic asks what IS this.

Multiple people looking at the exact same object have completely different inner worlds. A Georgian manor house: one person sees nostalgia, another envy, another horror, another does the math on the mortgage. Same outer stimulus. Four different inner realities.

"Perception is the only reality for the person involved." Not a platitude — a design feature.

"There may be more danger in prejudices which are apparently founded in logic than in those which are acknowledged as emotions." The dangerous biases are the ones that feel rational.

"PO is the laxative of language." It relaxes rigid mental structures without affirming or denying anything.

PO appears in: hyPOthesis, supPOse, POssible, POetry. He noticed this. He didn't coin the sound — he just recognized it was already doing a job language had no name for.

"The vertical thinker says: I know what I am looking for. The lateral thinker says: I am looking but I won't know what I am looking for until I have found it."

"Vertical thinking concentrates and excludes what is irrelevant. Lateral thinking welcomes chance intrusions."

"Removing the faults in a stage-coach may produce a perfect stage-coach, but it is unlikely to produce the first motor car."

At least 40% of good provocations should be COMPLETELY unusable. If your provocations are safe, they won't work. The uselessness is the point. A safe provocation is a failed provocation.

"PO: the factory is downstream of itself." Makes no physical sense. But if you follow it: a factory forced to draw its water input from a point DOWNSTREAM of its own waste output would have to clean its own pollution. This idea later became law in some countries. The impossible statement generated real policy.

"PO: cars should have square wheels." The immediate observation: square wheels would produce very PREDICTABLE bumps. Predictable bumps → suspension can be designed to compensate → ACTIVE SUSPENSION (which became real technology). Also: with square wheels you wouldn't need a handbrake when parking. This leads to a special brake system for heavy vehicles — wheels that don't normally touch the ground but touch down for emergency stops.

One completely absurd impossible statement. Two real technologies.

"Unhappiness is best defined as the difference between our talents and our expectations."

"The image that concerns most people is the reflection they see in other people's minds."

"An expert is someone who has succeeded in making decisions and judgements simpler." (Not more complex — simpler.)

"The mind can only see what it is prepared to see."

"You can analyse the past, but you need to design the future."

"The minds of adults are full of patterns, so the creativity of innocence does not work. We have to develop techniques for getting out of these established patterns."

"Creativity involves breaking out of established patterns in order to look at things in a different way." — not unusual as a sentence, but the subtext is grim: if you don't use deliberate technique, patterns own you forever.

Nobel physicist Murray Gell-Mann said de Bono had "stumbled upon processes ten years before the mathematicians" described self-organizing systems formally. A medical doctor from Malta, with no formal math training, independently arrived at Hebb's Law and self-organizing systems through pure observation.

"Vertical thinking is like digging the same hole deeper. Lateral thinking is digging a hole in a different place." The only escape is lateral movement. More effort in the same direction is not escape.

"Reasoning is a computer simulation in the head of what might happen if the situation were real." Reasoning is not reality — it is a model of reality. The model can be perfectly logical and still wrong if the premises are wrong.

He proposed replacing language itself with number codes because language carries too much historical baggage. 6/2 = "Give me your point of view and I'll give you mine." 10/24 = Creative mood; inspired state. He genuinely thought this could work. He was not joking.

"Socratic argumentation is intrinsically fascist in nature." His words, his argument: the structure of debate where one side must win and one must lose is a power structure, not a truth-finding structure.

"Democracy is an excellent way of ensuring that nothing much gets done."

"If you never change your mind, why have one?"

"Everyone has the right to doubt everything as often as he pleases and the duty to do it at least once."

"Asking a question is the simplest way of focusing thinking." Not the fanciest. The simplest.

"A question is a polite way of demanding something."

"Imperfect analogies are particularly valuable because their disconnects spark insights." Where the analogy breaks down is exactly where the new idea lives.

The stepping stone: use an idea NOT as a solution but as a bridge. The bridge itself may be worthless. You throw it away once you get to the other side.

Fractionation: break a problem into parts, but NOT in the obvious logical way. Arbitrary decomposition forces new patterns. Logical decomposition just follows existing ones.

"You cannot look in a new direction by looking harder in the same direction."

"Sometimes the situation is only a problem because it is looked at in a certain way. Looked at in another way, the right course of action may be so obvious that the problem no longer exists."

"We need creativity in order to break free from the temporary structures that have been set up by a particular sequence of experience." The structures are temporary. They don't feel temporary. But they are.

"It is better to have enough ideas for some of them to be wrong, than to be always right by having no ideas at all."

"If you wait for opportunities to occur, you will be one of the crowd."

"Schools waste two-thirds of the talent in society. Universities sterilise the rest."

"Perception is real even when it is not reality."
