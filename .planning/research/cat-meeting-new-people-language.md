Raw language, real descriptions, real terminology from cat owners, forums, shelters, and veterinary behaviorists.

## 1. THE OVERLY SOCIAL CAT — Owner Descriptions of Embarrassing Friendliness

### The Betrayal / Disloyalty Frame
People describe their cat's social friendliness as a personal affront:
- "My cat is a flirt" / "such a flirt"
- "He has absolutely zero loyalty"
- "She sold me out" / "complete traitor"
- "Would leave me for anyone with a free hand"
- "Acts like he's never been pet before in his life"
- "Acts like I starve him" — performing neglect for sympathy pets from strangers
- "I pay the bills and THIS is how you repay me"
- The "sad neglected cat" routine — performing abandonment for visitors
- "She thinks every visitor came specifically to see HER"
- Cats described as "attention whores" (extremely common phrasing)
- "No loyalty whatsoever"
- "She loves everyone more than me"

### The Social Butterfly Descriptions
- "My cat greets everyone like a Walmart greeter"
- "He's basically the mayor of the neighborhood"
- "She's the welcoming committee"
- "He thinks he's a golden retriever"
- "Acts more like a dog than a cat"
- "Has never met a stranger in her life"
- "Life of the party"
- "She insists on being the first AND last to greet anyone who comes within 20 feet of the house"
- "Not only greets everybody, but if they have a bag, or tools, or a purse, he goes to investigate"
- Bolts toward the front door meowing their heads off when someone knocks

### The Embarrassment Factor
- Cat described as "too friendly" — owners genuinely worried it's a problem
- "I'm embarrassed by how friendly he is"
- Friends say "they don't like to come over because of his behavior" — cat pushing into laps, sniffing crotches
- Cat performing "sad neglected cat routine" for visitors — meowing like it's been abandoned, owners mortified
- One cat described as jumping up on armrest where a guest was sitting "to check out the newcomer and get some pets"

## 2. THE "CHECKING OUT" BEHAVIORS — What Cats Actually DO

### The Inspection Protocol
- The sniff inspection — nose-to-everything investigation
- "Full body scan" — systematic sniffing from shoes up
- Cats rubbing their face on visitors' legs and doing "figure eights, curling his tail around your ankles"
- Circling ankles — the weave pattern, figure-eight through legs
- "He rubs his face on your legs and does figure eights"
- Jumping in laps uninvited — "within two seconds of sitting down"
- Following visitors to the bathroom
- Sitting on their stuff — bags, coats, laptops
- Investigating bags, tools, purses
- "All up in their business"
- "Has no concept of personal space"
- "Zero respect for boundaries"
- "They don't know the meaning of personal space"
- Cat sitting IN the plumber's toolbox
- Tapping the plumber on the head (Sushi the Siamese, viral video — "just supervising the work")
- Senior cat Arthur sitting next to plumber's feet meowing continuously
- Cat performing "quality control" or "project manager" role with repair workers

### The Approach Sequence
- The slow stroll — "casually strolled over" pretending not to care
- The fake disinterest followed by sudden proximity
- Sitting nearby watching, then gradually moving closer
- The "I'm going to pretend I don't care but I'm absolutely watching you" dance
- "She sat at the edge of the room for about ten minutes, then she was in his lap"
- The strategic placement — sitting where they HAVE to notice
- Cats appearing from nowhere the moment someone sits down

### The Greeting Ritual
- Running to the door when the doorbell rings
- "Legs it to the door" / "flies to the door"
- Some cats can distinguish between family footsteps and stranger sounds
- Recognizing specific sounds like a van door closing
- The meow greeting — specific chirps and trills reserved for arrivals
- "Tail straight up like an antenna" — the cat equivalent of a wave

## 3. CAT BODY LANGUAGE OF INTEREST — The Physical Vocabulary

### Positive Approach Signals
- **Tail up** — "Tail held high, often with a tiny curve at the very end" = happy to see you
- **Question mark tail** — tail relaxed with a hook at the top, "willing to interact"
- **Ears forward** — engaged, interested, curious
- **Slow blink** — "the cat equivalent of a kiss" — trust signal
- **Slow approach** — deliberate, unhurried movement toward person
- **Elevator butt** — rear end rising high when patted, "a declaration of trust"
- **Head bonk / head boop** — pressing forehead against you
- **Side rub** — pressing full body length against legs
- **Belly show** — "relaxed, stretched-out body with an exposed belly means trust — but don't assume it's an invitation for belly rubs"

### The Physical Affection Behaviors
- **Bunting** — pressing head and face against you to deposit scent
- **Kneading / making biscuits** — "padding and clawing, stepping on one paw and then the other, as if kneading dough, usually because they're content"
- **Purring** — the motorboat / "purr machine" / "the motor kicks in"
- **Drooling** — sign of extreme contentment (unlike dogs, rare without reason)
- **Love bites** — gentle nibbling, "the feline equivalent of a peck on the cheek"
- **Face plant** — pressing face directly into you
- **Chirping / trilling** — specific greeting vocalization, "a sign they're happy to see you"
- **The butt presentation** — "sticking their bum in your face" as a greeting/trust display

### The "I'm Watching You" Spectrum
- **Side eye** — "eyes half-closed with dilated pupils" = not into it
- **Airplane ears** — ears flattened and rotated, "negative emotions," anxiety around strangers
- **Halloween cat** — arched back, puffed tail, defensive posture
- **Loaf position** — crouched, tucked in, watchful but not engaged
- **Fake sleeping** — eyes closed but ears rotating, tracking sounds

## 5. VET / ANIMAL BEHAVIORIST TERMINOLOGY — Technical Terms That Sound Hilarious

### Core Technical Vocabulary
- **Allorubbing** — "bunting done in a social context." Rubbing faces/bodies against each other to mix scents and create a "group social odor which reassures individuals that the social group is stable"
- **Allogrooming** — one cat grooming another, typically head and neck. "Higher ranking animals groomed lower ranking animals more often"
- **Bunting** — head-pressing behavior using scent glands on cheeks, forehead, chin, ears. "Cat-to-cat head bunting and allorubbing are reserved for cats who already have a familiar and friendly relationship"
- **Social facilitation** — scientifically recognized phenomenon leveraged for group dynamics
- **Solicitation purr** — "produced when the cat 'wants something' such as food or attention, primarily directed at human care givers"
- **Piloerection** — fur standing on end, "maximizes the apparent size of the body as part of threat or defensive displays"
- **Olfactory investigation** — scent-based information gathering about security of environment and social group
- **Affiliative behaviors** — the category for friendly social actions: "allogrooming and allorubbing are used to cement relationships and exchange scent signals"
- **Proximity seeking** — cats spending "significantly more time in proximity to the attentive human"
- **Social referencing** — using another's emotional cues to guide behavior
- **Tactile communication** — physical touch-based interaction
- **Social roll** — rolling in front of a partner, part of behavioral clusters
- **Socially flexible species** — cats described as having "the capacity for social affiliation that readily extends to interactions with humans"
- **Care-soliciting call** — the purr as "a friendly greeting that typically occurs during amicable social interactions"
- **Displacement-like behaviors** — behavioral patterns observed alongside affiliative ones during greetings
- **Tail-up posture** — technical term for the greeting flag, measured by "duration of tail-up posture" in studies

### Scent Gland Locations (for maximum comedy)
- Cheeks, forehead, chin, ears
- Perianal area
- Between the digits (between the TOES)
- "When cats rub their face or tail against humans, they are leaving pheromones behind"
- "He's not just greeting you; he's marking you as his"

### Research Findings
- Cats differentiate between owner's voice and stranger's voice
- Greeting vocalizations in domestic cats are "more frequent with male caregivers" (2026 study)
- Cats use multimodal communication — vocal, visual, tactile, and olfactory simultaneously
- The purr is both a greeting AND a manipulation tool (solicitation purr)
- Related cats show more affiliative behaviors than unrelated cats within groups

## 7. BONUS: THE PHYSICAL COMEDY OF CAT GREETINGS

### The Tripping Hazard
- "Figure eights around your feet"
- "Ankle weaving" — zig-zagging between legs
- "Especially hazardous when you're balancing a pile of laundry or carrying a mug of hot coffee"
- "A common (and sometimes hazardous!) behaviour that's equal parts endearing and inconvenient"
- Tail curling around ankles
- "Walking shoulder to shoulder" — how bonded cats walk together

### The Unwanted Inspection
- Cat sniffing crotches — "Buster will often push his way into a guest's lap and start sniffing"
- "Friends say they don't like to come over because of his behavior"
- The butt-in-face greeting — presenting their posterior as introduction
- "Sticking their bum in your face" = trust display, similar to dog butt-sniff greeting

### Cat Sounds of Greeting
- **Meow** — primarily used for human communication, rarely cat-to-cat
- **Chirp** — "a way of your cat saying hi"
- **Trill** — "greeting or acknowledging you, expressing happiness"
- **Purr** — "friendly greeting and care-soliciting call"
- **Chatter** — excitement sounds
- The specific meow that sounds like "hello" — viral video genre (Onyx greeting Amazon drivers)
