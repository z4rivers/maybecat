## 3. LOVE ISLAND — The Complete Slang Glossary

### The Core Vocabulary

**Grafting** — A step above flirting. Putting in the WORK to win someone over. "I need to put my grafting boots on." "You need to graft me."

**Crack on** — To get on with it, pursue someone romantically. The goal of every islander. The most compatible couples "cracked on really well."

**Mugged off** — Treated disrespectfully or deceived. Deceived and disrespected in one go.

**Muggy** — Disloyal, snakey. "A disloyal friend is then deemed muggy, and their reputation almost never recovers."

**Pied / Pied off** — Dumped, ditched, abandoned. Being dumped or rebuffed while grafting.

**Melt** — Someone embarrassingly pining after another person. "Someone who has a lot of feelings for someone and is willing to come across a fool to prove it."

**My type on paper** — Your ideal person in theory. "If you were to make a list of everything you want in a partner." Often followed by a "but" because the chemistry isn't there.

**100% my type on paper** — The emphatic version.

**Head turned** — When someone in a couple becomes attracted to a new person. "Tends to happen a lot when a bombshell comes in or during Casa Amor."

**The ick** — When attraction dies, often suddenly and irreversibly. Once you've caught it, it's over.

**Catch feelings** — To develop romantic feelings (often unintentionally).

**Coupled up** — Officially paired with someone.

**Dead ting** — Someone deemed extremely dull. (Made famous by Amber Gill.)

**Bev** — A hot guy. "If he's your boyfriend he's your bev, but if he's not your boyfriend, he's a bev."

**Salty** — Bitter or angry toward another islander.

**Snake / Snakey** — Crafty, sneaky, backstabber.

**Loyal** — Georgia Steel's favorite word. Used to reassure partners their heads won't turn.

**Factor 50** — When a guy tries WAY too hard with a girl. Going all factor 50 on them.

**Do bits** — Sexual activities. Created by Wes Nelson (S4). The "Do Bits Society" or "DBS" was a secret members' club for islanders who had successfully... done bits.

**Getting to know** — Code for "I fancy you." If someone says they want to "get to know you," they're interested.

**It is what it is** — The universal Love Island acceptance phrase.

**I've got a text!** — Shouted when a phone buzzes with a show announcement.

**Can I pull you for a chat?** — Relays interest without coming on too strong. Wanting private time away from other contestants.

## 7. SPEED DATING — The Three-Minute Assessment

### The Opening Formula
- "Hi, I am... It's so nice to meet you." — then first question
- In 59% of dates, males spoke up first. This worked best.
- The weirder the greeting, the worse the chances.

### Classic Speed Dating Questions
- "Have you speed dated before?"
- "What do you like most about your job?" (open-ended beats "what's your job?")
- "What's your go-to karaoke song?"
- "If you could travel anywhere, where would you go?"
- "Do you have pets?" — to identify animal lovers
- "Are you a dog person or a cat person?"

### Body Language That Means "Yes"
- Leaning in, touching, head tilts — highest success rate
- Females smiled more than males, and this worked
- Lip biting, touching neck, playing with glass — nervous excitement
- Prolonged eye contact (unless creepy)
- Pupil dilation when attracted

## 8. CROSS-SHOW VOCABULARY — The Universal Dating Show Language

### The Ringer's "Official Taxonomy of Dating Show Parlance" (2024)
Contestants across ALL shows recite the same script:
- "a family person with golden retriever energy" — describing what they want
- "The process has worked for so many people before, so I figured, why not me?" — why they're on the show
- "Can I pull you for a chat?" — universal interest signal

### Emotional Vocabulary (Across All Shows)
- "I felt a connection"
- "There was a spark"
- "The chemistry was there"
- "I felt it immediately"
- "You caught my eye"
- "I'm not usually one to..."
- "Something about you"
- "I can't explain it"
- "I've got my walls up" — appearing more and more in dating shows
- "Emotionally available" / "emotionally unavailable"
- "Opening up"
- "Being vulnerable"
- "Guarded"
- "I'm ready to find my person"

### The Checking-Out Language (Sizing Someone Up)
- "You're gorgeous"
- "You're not what I expected" (positive or negative)
- "I like your energy"
- "My type"
- "I've got a type and you're it"
- "Not my usual type, but..."
- "Pleasantly surprised"
- "I like what I see"
- "The moment I saw you..."
- "You gave me butterflies"
- "We just clicked"

### The Rejection Language
- "I just don't see it"
- "I don't think we're compatible"
- "I got the ick"
- "My head's been turned" (by someone else)
- "I need to follow my heart"
- "I don't know if I can get there"
- "I think we're better as friends"
