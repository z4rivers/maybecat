## NASA / SPACE LAUNCHES

### Apollo 11 Go/No-Go Poll (Lunar Descent)
Gene Kranz polled each controller before the lunar landing:
- **"OK, all flight controllers, go/no go for landing."** - Gene Kranz, Flight Director
- **"Go."** - RETRO (Retrofire Officer)
- **"Go."** - FIDO (Flight Dynamics Officer)
- **"Go."** - GUIDANCE
- **"Go."** - CONTROL
- **"Go."** - INCO
- **"Go."** - GNC
- **"Go."** - EECOM
- **"Go."** - Surgeon
- Then CAPCOM Charlie Duke radioed: **"Eagle, Houston. You're GO for landing. Over."**
- Armstrong responded: **"Roger. Understand. GO for landing."**
Source: [Apollo 11 Transcripts - NASA](https://www.hq.nasa.gov/alsj/a11/a11trans.html), [ABC News - Gene Kranz recalls final moments](https://abcnews.go.com/Politics/50-years-apollo-11-flight-director-recalls-final/story?id=64228724)

### Apollo 11 Launch Status Check

- **"GNC?" "Go, Flight."**
- **"TELCOM?" "Go, Flight."**

Source: [Launch status check - Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Launch_status_check)

### Apollo 11 Landing
- **"Houston, Tranquility Base here. The Eagle has landed."** - Neil Armstrong, spontaneously naming the site
Source: [National Archives transcript](https://www.archives.gov/files/social-media/transcripts/transcript-eagle-has-landed-1969-45017.pdf)

### Apollo 13 - The Real Quote vs. The Movie
- ACTUAL words (Jack Swigert): **"Okay, Houston... we've had a problem here."**
- Then Jim Lovell repeated: **"Ah, Houston, we've had a problem."** (past tense)
- The movie changed it to present tense: "Houston, we have a problem"
Source: [Houston, we have a problem - Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Houston,_we_have_a_problem)

### Gene Kranz - "Failure Is Not an Option"
- **He never actually said it.** The line was written for the 1995 Apollo 13 film.
- The real origin: FIDO Flight Controller Jerry Bostick told screenwriters: **"No, when bad things happened, we just calmly laid out all the options, and failure was not one of them."**
- Kranz liked it enough to title his memoir after it.
Source: [Failure is not an option - Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Failure_is_not_an_option), [Houston Public Media](https://www.houstonpublicmedia.org/articles/shows/houston-matters/2023/08/29/460817/gene-kranz-never-said-failure-is-not-an-option-but-his-real-legacy-is-tough-and-competent/)

### NASA Flight Director Authority
- **"There's only one flight director. From the moment the mission starts until the moment the crew is safe on board a recovery ship, I'm in charge."**
Source: [Flight controller - Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flight_controller)

### Space Shuttle Launch Sequence
- T-minus 10 seconds: **"Go for main engine start"**
- T-minus 6 seconds: Each of three main engines ignites sequentially
- T-minus 0: SRB ignition, explosive bolt release
- **"And we have liftoff."**
Source: [Space.com - Shuttle Launch Steps](https://www.space.com/12186-space-shuttle-launch-countdown-minutes-liftoff.html)

### SpaceX Falcon 9 Launch Callouts
- **"LD is go for launch."** (Launch Director's final authorization)
- **"Falcon 9 is in startup."** (autonomous flight computers take over)
- **"Main engine cut off."** (MECO)
- **"Stage separation confirmed."**
Source: [SpaceX launch transcripts](https://podcasts.happyscribe.com/fox-news/spacex-falcon-9-rocket-launches)

## MILITARY ORDERS & FAMOUS CHARGES

### D-Day - Eisenhower's Actual Order (June 5, 1944)
- The actual words to his deputies: **"O.K. Let's go."**
- The formal Order of the Day distributed to troops: **"You are about to embark upon the Great Crusade."**
Source: [National Archives - Eisenhower's Order of the Day](https://www.archives.gov/milestone-documents/general-eisenhowers-order-of-the-day), [Eisenhower Presidential Library](https://www.eisenhowerlibrary.gov/research/online-documents/world-war-ii-d-day-invasion-normandy)

### Gettysburg - Pickett's Charge (July 3, 1863)
- Pickett: **"Up, men, and to your posts! Don't forget today that you are from old Virginia!"**
- Armistead (before the charge): **"Look at my line. It never looked better on dress parade."**
- Armistead (during, just before being shot): **"Give them cold steel, boys!"**
- Pettigrew to a colonel: **"Now, colonel, for the honor of the good old North State, forward!"**
- Union troops began shouting: **"Give them Fredericksburg!"**
Source: [American Battlefield Trust - Pickett's Charge](https://www.battlefields.org/learn/articles/picketts-charge)

### Little Round Top - Chamberlain's Bayonet Charge (July 2, 1863)
- When ammunition ran out, Chamberlain ordered: **"Bayonet!"**
- Possibly also: **"Bayonet! Forward to the right!"**
- Chamberlain later claimed the single word **"Bayonet!"** was enough -- no one could hear anything more over the noise.
Source: [American Battlefield Trust - Defense of Little Round Top](https://www.battlefields.org/learn/articles/defense-little-round-top)

### Stonewall Jackson at Bull Run
- **"Up, men! Up, Virginians! Hold your fire until they are within fifty yards, and then give them the bayonet! And when you charge, yell like furies!"**
Source: [The Leader Maker - Fix Bayonets](https://www.theleadermaker.com/fix-bayonets-famous-leader-commands/)

### Battle of Bunker Hill (1775)
- Famous version: **"Don't fire until you see the whites of their eyes!"**
- Attribution disputed between Prescott, Putnam, Stark, and Gridley -- may have been said by one and repeated by the others
- Modern scholarly consensus: probably nobody said it at the battle; it's a legend borrowed from Swedish King Gustavus Adolphus (1594-1632) who ordered musketeers **"never to give fire, till they could see their own image in the pupil of their enemy's eye"**
Source: [Journal of the American Revolution](https://allthingsliberty.com/2020/06/who-said-dont-fire-till-you-see-the-whites-of-their-eyes/)

### Admiral Farragut at Mobile Bay (1864)
- Famous paraphrase: "Damn the torpedoes! Full speed ahead!"
- Most likely actual words: **"Damn the torpedoes. Four bells, Captain Drayton."** Then to another commander: **"Go ahead, Jouett, full speed."**
- Another primary source version: **"Damn the torpedoes! Go on! Put the helm a-starboard, Captain Drayton!"**
- Some men present doubted any such communication could be heard above the din of the guns.
Source: [Naval History Magazine - USNI](https://www.usni.org/magazines/naval-history-magazine/2014/july/damn-torpedoes), [American Battlefield Trust](https://www.battlefields.org/learn/articles/damn-torpedoes)

### Nelson at Trafalgar (October 21, 1805)
- Signal sent at 11:45 AM via flag code: **"England expects that every man will do his duty."**
- Nelson originally wanted to say **"Nelson confides that every man will do his duty"** but was convinced to change "Nelson" to "England" and "confides" to "expects" (because "expects" was in the signal codebook; "confides" would need to be spelled out letter by letter).
- The word "duty" also had to be spelled out, requiring 12 flag "lifts" taking about 4 minutes.
- His final signal, sent at 12:15 PM: **"Engage the enemy more closely."** It flew at the masthead until it was shot away.
Source: [England expects - Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/England_expects_that_every_man_will_do_his_duty)

### Chesty Puller at Chosin Reservoir (1950)
- **"So they've got us surrounded, good! Now we can fire in any direction, those bastards won't get away this time!"**
- Alternative version: **"We're surrounded. That simplifies the problem."**
- Another version: **"All right, they're on our left, they're on our right, they're in front of us, they're behind us... they can't get away this time."**
- From Burke Davis biography: **"Those poor bastards. They've got us right where we want 'em. We can shoot in every direction now."**
Source: [AZ Quotes - Chesty Puller](https://www.azquotes.com/quote/553432), [Goodreads - Marine! The Life of Chesty Puller](https://www.goodreads.com/work/quotes/191783-marine-the-life-of-chesty-puller)

### Grant at Fort Donelson (1862)
- **"No terms except an unconditional and immediate surrender can be accepted. I propose to move immediately upon your works."**
- The "unconditional surrender" phrase was actually first used by Flag Officer Foote at Fort Henry: **"No sir, your surrender will be unconditional!"**
- And by Gen. Smith earlier the same day: **"I'll have no terms with Rebels with guns in their hands, my terms are unconditional and immediate surrender."**
Source: [American Battlefield Trust - Grant at Fort Donelson](https://www.battlefields.org/learn/articles/ulysses-s-grant-myth-unconditional-surrender-begins-fort-donelson)

### Patton's Speech to the Third Army (June 5, 1944)
- **"We're not just going to shoot the bastards, we're going to rip out their living goddamned guts and use them to grease the treads of our tanks."**
- **"No bastard ever won a war by dying for his country."** (from May 31, 1944 version to 6th Armored Division)
- Patton delivered without notes; the order of sections varied each time.
Source: [Patton's Speech to the Third Army - Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_S._Patton's_speech_to_the_Third_Army), [Task & Purpose](https://taskandpurpose.com/history/6-remarkable-excerpts-from-pattons-famously-vulgar-3rd-army-speech/)

### Thermopylae - Leonidas (480 BC)
- **"Molon labe"** ("Come and take them") - Leonidas's response when told to lay down arms. BUT: first mention is from Plutarch, 500+ years after the battle. No near-contemporary accounts.
- **"Then we shall fight in the shade"** - actually attributed by Herodotus to the soldier Dienekes, not Leonidas, in response to a warning that Persian arrows would block the sun.
- To his wife Gorgo before leaving: **"Marry brave men, and bear them brave children."**
Source: [Leonidas I - Wikiquote](https://en.wikiquote.org/wiki/Leonidas_I), [Bad Ancient - Molon Labe](https://www.badancient.com/claims/molon-labe/)

### Caesar Crossing the Rubicon (49 BC)
- Famous Latin: **"Alea iacta est"** ("The die is cast")
- But Plutarch says he said it in Greek, quoting Menander: **"Anerrhiphtho kybos"** ("Let the die be cast") - a self-encouragement, not a statement of fate
- Some historians say he may not have said it at all
Source: [Alea iacta est - Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alea_iacta_est)

### Churchill - "We Shall Fight on the Beaches" (June 4, 1940)
- **"We shall go on to the end, we shall fight in France, we shall fight on the seas and oceans, we shall fight with growing confidence and growing strength in the air, we shall defend our Island, whatever the cost may be, we shall fight on the beaches, we shall fight on the landing grounds, we shall fight in the fields and in the streets, we shall fight in the hills; we shall never surrender."**
Source: [International Churchill Society](https://winstonchurchill.org/resources/speeches/1940-the-finest-hour/we-shall-fight-on-the-beaches/)

### Roosevelt - Declaration of War (December 8, 1941)
- **"I ask that the Congress declare that since the unprovoked and dastardly attack by Japan on Sunday, December 7th, 1941, a state of war has existed between the United States and the Japanese empire."**
Source: [National Archives](https://www.archives.gov/milestone-documents/joint-address-to-congress-declaration-of-war-against-japan)

### Todd Beamer - Flight 93 (September 11, 2001)
- Actual words heard by GTE supervisor Lisa Jefferson: **"Are you ready? Okay. Let's roll."**
Source: [Let's roll - Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Let's_roll)

### Operation Neptune Spear - Bin Laden Raid (May 2, 2011)
- SEAL team leader's radio transmission: **"For God and country -- Geronimo, Geronimo, Geronimo."**
Source: [Killing of Osama bin Laden - Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Killing_of_Osama_bin_Laden)

### Pearl Harbor - Fuchida's Radio Transmission (December 7, 1941)
- **"Tora! Tora! Tora!"** (Tiger! Tiger! Tiger!) - code for "Attack, surprise achieved"
Source: [Attack on Pearl Harbor timeline](https://www.spokesman.com/stories/2024/dec/07/timeline-of-the-attack-on-pearl-harbor/)

### World War I - Going "Over the Top"
- Officers blew a whistle (Acme Thunderer, 3-4 kHz frequency) to cut through artillery noise
- The whistle WAS the "go" command -- verbal orders couldn't be heard over the barrage
- Thousands of men would stream out of the trench and run across No Man's Land
Source: [National WWI Museum - Whistles](https://www.theworldwar.org/learn/about-wwi/whistles), [War History Online](https://www.warhistoryonline.com/instant-articles/whistles-used-in-the-trenches.html)

## FIGHTER PILOT / AIR COMBAT RADIO

### Fox Codes (Weapon Release)
- **"Fox One"** - semi-active radar guided missile (AIM-7 Sparrow)
- **"Fox Two"** - infrared-guided missile (heat seeker)
- **"Fox Three"** - active radar guided missile (AIM-120 AMRAAM)
- "Fox" is short for "Foxtrot" (F), which is short for "Fire"
Source: [Fox (code word) - Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fox_(code_word))

### Other Brevity Codes
- **"Tally"** - enemy sighted (opposite of "No joy" = can't see them)
- **"Visual"** - friendly aircraft sighted
- **"Bogey"** - unidentified radar/visual contact
- **"Bandit"** - confirmed hostile aircraft
- **"Splash one"** - one target destroyed
- **"Bingo"** - fuel state needed for recovery / proceeding to base
- **"Winchester"** - all ordnance expended (out of weapons)
Source: [Multi-service tactical brevity code - Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multi-service_tactical_brevity_code)

### Close Air Support
- **"Cleared hot"** - JTAC authorization to release weapons on target
- Ground controller says **"Attack"** to authorize bombing run; pilot replies **"Attacking"**
- **"Shot"** - artillery has fired the requested rounds
- **"Splash"** - round expected to hit target in 5 seconds
- Important: military NEVER says **"repeat"** on radio; use **"say again"** -- because "repeat" means "resume firing weapons"
Source: [We Are The Mighty - Cleared Hot](https://www.wearethemighty.com/mighty-stories/cleared-hot/), [We Are The Mighty - 13 radio calls](https://www.wearethemighty.com/popular/13-radio-calls-troops-really-love-to-hear/)

### Danger Close / Broken Arrow
- **"Danger close"** - friendly forces within 600m of target (mortar/artillery), 750m (5-inch naval), 1000m (larger naval)
- **"Broken Arrow"** - American unit facing imminent destruction; ALL available aircraft diverted for immediate air support. Most famously used at Battle of Ia Drang, Vietnam.
Source: [Fire discipline - Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fire_discipline), [Battle of Ia Drang - Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Ia_Drang)

### Weapons Free vs. Weapons Tight
- **"Weapons free"** - authorized to fire on any non-friendly targets
- **"Weapons tight"** - restricted to pre-designated targets or imminent threats only
Source: [NATO Brevity Codes](https://airpra.com/nato-active-air-combat-terminology/)

## AVIATION (CIVILIAN)

### ATC Takeoff Clearance
- **"Cherokee 8121K, Runway 26, cleared for takeoff."** Pilot: **"Cleared for takeoff Runway 26, Cherokee 8121K."**
- **"Fly heading two three zero, Runway two seven Left, cleared for takeoff."**
- Critical distinction: **"Line up and wait"** = taxi onto runway but DO NOT take off. Await further clearance.
- Pilots must read back all controller instructions.
Source: [Thrust Flight - ATC Communications](https://www.thrustflight.com/air-traffic-control-communications-every-pilot-must-know/), [AOPA - New Pilot's Guide](https://www.aopa.org/training-and-safety/students/presolo/special/new-pilots-guide-to-atc-communication)

## SUBMARINE COMMANDS

### Dive Sequence
- **"NOW RIG SHIP FOR DIVE"** - first order, watch-standers carry out valve and ventilation lineups
- Captain: **"Diving Officer, submerge the ship to [depth]."**
- Chief of the Watch sounds the diving alarm (the "ah-OOG-ah" klaxon)
- Announcement over intercom: **"Dive, dive."** (alarm sounded twice)
- Emergency surfacing: alarm sounded three times, announcement **"Surface, surface, surface."**
Source: [Medium - "Now Rig Ship for Dive"](https://medium.com/@kyxcprc/now-rig-ship-for-dive-57293ac4b939), [Alarms on submarines - Military Wiki](https://military-history.fandom.com/wiki/Alarms_on_submarines_of_the_United_States_Navy)

### Torpedo Firing
- **"Standby"** - preparatory order (never "standby to fire" to avoid premature firing if only part heard)
- **"Fire [tube number]"** - passed over intercom to communications and firing number
- "FIRE" displayed on illuminated Order Instrument for each tube
Source: [USS Pampanito - Torpedo Firing Procedure](https://maritime.org/tech/torpfire.php)

## NAVAL COMMANDS

### Taking Command
- **"I have the conn."** - formal verbal announcement; must be acknowledged with **"You have the conn."** Helmsman and bridge team confirm.
- The conn slots are positioned so one person cannot reach both at once.
Source: [Conn (nautical) - Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conn_(nautical))

## NUCLEAR LAUNCH AUTHORIZATION

### Presidential Authorization
1. President convenes conference with military/civilian advisers
2. President opens the "biscuit" (credit-card-sized plastic card with Gold Codes)
3. Authentication: NMCC deputy director issues challenge of two phonetic letters; president reads matching letters from biscuit
4. Attack option and Gold Codes transmitted to NMCC via special secure channel

### At the Silo (Minuteman III)
1. Launch crews compare Sealed Authentication System (SAS) codes in the launch order against codes in their safes
2. Safe has TWO separate locks -- each officer has key to only ONE lock
3. Each officer has one of two launch keys
4. **Both keys must be turned within 2 seconds of each other** ("simultaneously")
5. Key slots positioned far enough apart that one person CANNOT reach both
6. **Two launch votes required** (from two separate launch control centers) = minimum 4 officers turning keys to launch
Source: [Two-person rule - Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Two-man_rule), [Gold Codes - Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gold_Codes), [Association of Air Force Missileers](https://www.afmissileers.org/Minuteman-Launch)

## MEDICAL / SURGICAL

### WHO Surgical Safety Checklist - "Time Out"
- Performed immediately before incision, with surgeon present, involving ALL team members
- Confirm: patient name, procedure, incision site
- Confirm: prophylactic antibiotics administered within 60 min before incision
- Confirm: essential imaging displayed for correct patient
- Anticipate critical events
- Questions read aloud FROM the list, not from memory
Source: [WHO Surgical Safety Checklist - Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WHO_Surgical_Safety_Checklist)

### Surgical Language
- Circulating nurse announces: **"Before we make the skin incision"** (part of WHO checklist)
- Some surgeons call out **"Incision"** when starting -- not standard, but used for time documentation
- The circulator documents the time of incision among other critical times
- During critical moments, the OR goes silent with all focus on the procedure
Source: [in-Training - OR Terminology](https://in-training.org/a-beginners-guide-to-or-terminology-18784)

## SWAT / TACTICAL ENTRY

### Breach Commands (Sequential)
- **"Open and Clear"** - approach, check, open door, clear
- **"Move and Clear"** - approach open hostile doorway
- **"Grenade and Clear"** - grenade into hostile doorway first
- **"Demo and Clear"** - explosive breach on closed door, then clear
- Standard sequence: **"Breach, Bang, Clear"** (breach door, throw flashbang, clear room)
Source: [Tactical Operations Center Wiki](https://tacticaloperationscenter.fandom.com/wiki/TOC_TTP_Breaching_Techniques)

## LEGAL / OFFICIAL

### Execution Protocol
- Warden reads the death warrant aloud to the condemned immediately before execution
- Condemned given opportunity for final statement
- In Texas: the warden removes his glasses -- this is the signal to executioners behind mirrored glass; when glasses come off, the lethal injection begins to flow
Source: [Execution warrant - Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Execution_warrant)

### Courtroom Language
- **"Sustained"** / **"Overruled"** - judge's ruling on objections
- Judges use standardized legal language; addressing as "Your Honor"
Source: [Nevada Courts - Common Courtroom Phrases](https://nvcourts.gov/aoc/programs_and_services/court_interpreter/interpreter_resources/common_courtroom_phrases)

## BOXING / FIGHTING

### Boxing Referee Commands
- **"Seconds out"** - rest period over, trainers must leave the corner
- **"Box"** - command to begin fighting (start of each round)

## RACING

### Indianapolis 500 - Starting Command
- First said by circus barker Irish Horan in 1951 (possibly as "Gentlemen, start your MOTORS")
- First appeared in print on race morning countdown schedule in 1953
- Wilbur Shaw standardized: **"Gentlemen, start your engines!"** (1953-1954)

## DUELING

### Code Duello Protocol
- Firing could be regulated: by signal, by word of command, or at pleasure
- **"Present"** = take aim (anywhere from hip to eye level)
- Seconds load weapons in presence of each other
- The challenged chooses ground; challenger chooses distance; seconds fix time and terms
- No bending knee or covering side with left hand permitted
Source: [PBS - Code Duello](https://www.pbs.org/wgbh/americanexperience/features/duel-code-duello-rules-dueling/)

## SHIP CHRISTENING

- Traditional phrase: **"I christen thee [ship name]"** -- spoken while smashing a bottle of wine/champagne against the hull
- US Navy version: **"I christen thee United States Ship [name]"**
- Breaking a bottle over the bow became popular in late 1700s (British navy)
- Champagne began in the 1800s
Source: [NPS - Ship Ceremonies](https://www.nps.gov/articles/000/ship-ceremonies-cny.htm), [NARA image description of USS Stennis christening](https://nara.getarchive.net/media/with-the-words-i-christen-thee-united-states-ship-john-c-stennis-mrs-margaret-5e83ae)

## THE ALAMO (1836) - Line in the Sand

- Legend: Travis drew a line in the dirt with his sword, asking men to cross it if they'd stay and fight
- **The story was first published 40 years after the battle** (1873, by William P. Zuber)
- Supposedly came from Louis Rose, the man who escaped -- but Rose's name never appeared on the Alamo muster rolls
- Battle survivors Susanna Dickison and Joe (Travis's enslaved person) stated they never knew anyone named Louis Rose
- Most historians agree Travis DID inform the men their situation was dire; the line in the sand itself is likely legend
Source: [Line in the sand - Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Line_in_the_sand), [Atlas Obscura](https://www.atlasobscura.com/places/alamo-line-in-the-sand)
