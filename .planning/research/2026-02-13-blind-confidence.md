## 1. DUNNING-KRUGER HALL OF FAME

### The Lemon Juice Bank Robber (THE origin story)

McArthur Wheeler robbed two Pittsburgh banks on January 6, 1995, with his face covered in lemon juice, believing it would make him invisible to security cameras -- because lemon juice works as invisible ink. He had even "tested" it by taking a Polaroid selfie (which came out blank, likely because he pointed the camera wrong). When police showed him the surveillance footage, he was genuinely stunned:

**"But I wore the juice."** -- McArthur Wheeler, upon arrest, April 20, 1995
*Source: Pittsburgh Post-Gazette; later cited in Dunning & Kruger's 1999 Cornell paper "Unskilled and Unaware of It"*

This case literally inspired psychologists David Dunning and Justin Kruger to study whether "someone's perceived competence could be measured against their actual competence." Dunning's insight: "If Wheeler was too stupid to be a bank robber, perhaps he was also too stupid to know that he was too stupid to be a bank robber."

-- William Orton, President of Western Union, 1876
*Source: Internal Western Union memo; widely documented*

*Source: Documented in British postal service records*

**"I think there is a world market for maybe five computers."**
-- Thomas Watson, Chairman of IBM, 1943
*Source: Widely attributed; some dispute exact wording but sentiment documented*

**"There is no reason anyone would want a computer in their home."**
-- Ken Olsen, President/Chairman/Founder of Digital Equipment Corp. (DEC), 1977
*Source: Convention speech, documented in multiple contemporary accounts*

**"Fooling around with alternating current (AC) is just a waste of time. Nobody will use it, ever."**
-- Thomas Edison, 1889
*Source: Cited in Linda Zimmermann's "Bad Science" and "Electrical Genius Nikola Tesla" (Messner, 1959)*

**"The horse is here to stay but the automobile is only a novelty -- a fad."**
-- President of the Michigan Savings Bank, advising Henry Ford's lawyer Horace Rackham not to invest in Ford Motor Company, 1903
*Source: Sarah T. Bushnell's 1922 biography of Henry Ford, with Rackham's cooperation. Speaker identity unclear -- described only as "leading banker." Rackham invested anyway. In 1919, he sold his Ford stock for $12.5 million.*

-- Paul Krugman, Nobel Prize-winning economist, Red Herring magazine, June 10, 1998
*Source: Red Herring magazine, verified by Snopes and Quote Investigator. Krugman later said (2013) "the point was to be fun and provocative, not to engage in careful forecasting."*

## 5. ANIMAL CONFIDENCE

### Cats

Cats push things off tables **and science confirms it's deliberate.** A 2016 study in *Animal Cognition* by Saho Takagi et al. found cats understand cause-and-effect relationships with falling objects. They paid MORE attention when physics was violated. They aren't confused. They know EXACTLY what gravity does. They just don't care.
*Source: Animal Cognition journal, 2016*

Cat researcher Mikel Delgado: **"A lot of cats knock things over because they have learned it is a quick and easy way to get their human's attention."**
*Source: Hill's Pet, Bustle -- citing Delgado's research*

Cats' paws contain "many highly sensitive nerve receptors" used for hunting -- when they bat your coffee mug, they're using the same neural pathways they'd use to kill prey.
*Source: Creative Science; Cat in the Box LLC*

### The Chihuahua Principle

Chihuahuas regularly challenge Great Danes. One viral video shows a Chihuahua barking ferociously at a Great Dane through a glass door -- absolute war energy -- but when the door opens, it frantically retreats. The confidence was entirely predicated on the barrier.
*Source: Parade Pets; Yahoo Lifestyle*

Animal behaviorists call this **"small dog syndrome"** -- a "little bundle of insecurities that resolves this by deluding itself into thinking it's bigger and more powerful than it really is."
*Source: Quora animal behavior discussion, citing veterinary behaviorists*

### The Honey Badger

**"Honey badger don't care. Honey badger don't give a shit."**
-- Randall's viral narration over National Geographic footage, January 2011. Over 100 million views.
*Source: Time, Wikipedia. The honey badger genuinely does fight king cobras, raid beehives, and attack animals many times its size. The comedy is that the narration isn't wrong -- honey badgers actually are that reckless.*

### Deimatic Behavior (The Art of the Bluff)

**The frilled lizard** erects a frill to appear 4x its actual size, opens its mouth to show bright yellow/pink interior, rises on hind legs, hisses, and strikes the ground with its tail. **It is entirely bluffing.** It has no actual weapons. The whole display is a performance.
*Source: Wikipedia (Deimatic behaviour); One Earth; Environmental Literacy Council*

**Pufferfish** inflate to appear threatening -- but an inflated pufferfish can barely move and is actually MORE vulnerable while bluffing.
*Source: A-Z Animals; nature documentary compilations*

Geese will rush at barn cats, dogs, and sometimes even humans with absolute conviction. "A goose is just a goose, however brave, and no match for a desperate or determined predator." The confidence exceeds the capability every single time.
*Source: Permies forum; animal husbandry sources*

## PATTERNS FOR RELOCATION

### What Makes Blind Confidence Work as Found Language

1. **The gap between certainty and evidence.** Wheeler WORE THE JUICE. Ballmer LAUGHED at the iPhone. The Titanic VP said "absolutely unsinkable" while it was sinking. The wider the gap, the better the line.

2. **The doubling down.** Billy McFarland announced Fyre Festival II. Kanye called removing ego an act that only "I, Kanye West" could model. Napoleon invaded Russia AFTER dominating Europe. They don't just commit -- they recommit.

3. **The physics of confidence.** The frilled lizard is ALL display, no weapon. The chihuahua is confident BECAUSE of the glass door. The pufferfish becomes MORE vulnerable while bluffing. Confidence and capability are sometimes inversely correlated.

4. **Toddler epistemology.** The blanket might be cold. Dad IS Batman. The mouth does what it wants. Children aren't wrong about confidence -- they're wrong about everything else. The confidence is the only constant.

5. **The expert who is more wrong BECAUSE they're an expert.** Lord Kelvin was President of the Royal Society. Steve Ballmer ran Microsoft. Paul Krugman won a Nobel Prize. The credential amplifies the wrongness.

6. **"We were a little naive."** Understatement as confidence. McFarland built nothing on a beach and called it a festival. "A little naive" is doing so much work in that sentence.

- Cat as the one WITH the blind confidence (I knocked your coffee off the table. I'd do it again.)
