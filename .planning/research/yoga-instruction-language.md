# Yoga Instruction Language — Raw Material Research

**Date:** 2026-02-14
**Purpose:** Physical yoga instruction cues that accidentally work as life advice. NOT yoga philosophy. NOT spiritual platitudes. The actual BODY INSTRUCTIONS teachers say during class.
**Key insight:** The comedy/truth comes from the physicality. These are muscle and bone instructions that happen to describe emotional situations perfectly.

---

These are the things yoga teachers actually SAY during class — verbal instructions for the body that land differently when you hear them about your life.

- "Root down to rise up"
- "Soften what you don't need"

- "If you're breathing, you're doing it right"
- "Meet yourself where you are today"
- "Take what you need, leave what you don't"
- "Your only competition is yourself yesterday"

- "If you lost your breath, you went too far"
- "Soften your breathing"

## 2. POSE NAMES THAT WORK AS ADVICE

The names themselves are accidentally perfect.

### Warrior Series (Virabhadrasana I, II, III)
- **Warrior I** — face forward, arms up, commit to one direction
- **Warrior II** — look where you're going, extend in both directions
- **Warrior III** — balance requires you to lean ALL the way in
- Teacher cues: "Gaze past your front fingertips" / "Sink deeper into it" / "Find your strength in the stillness"
- The PROGRESSION: Warrior I (show up), Warrior II (hold your ground), Warrior III (trust yourself enough to fly)

### Child's Pose (Balasana)
- The universal retreat. Teachers literally say: "You can always come back to child's pose"
- "Rest when you need to" / "This is always available to you"
- Symbolizes surrender, nurturing, rest, vulnerability

### Corpse Pose (Savasana)
- The final pose. You literally practice dying.
- Teachers say it's "the hardest pose" because it requires doing NOTHING
- "The art of doing nothing is the hardest part"

### Tree Pose (Vrksasana)
- Balance requires constant micro-adjustments — you're never truly still
- Teacher cues: "Find your drishti" (focal point) / "Root through your standing foot" / "It's okay to wobble"
- "A tree that doesn't bend breaks"

### Mountain Pose (Tadasana)
- "Just standing, but REALLY standing"
- Teacher cues: "Stand like you mean it" / "Feel every inch of your height"
- "The simplest pose and the hardest to master"

### Cobra (Bhujangasana)
- Rising from the ground. Lifting your heart off the floor.
- "Lead with your chest" / "Rise only as high as your breath allows"
- Represents awakening, renewal, shedding old skin

### Pigeon Pose (Eka Pada Rajakapotasana)
- THE emotional release pose. Teachers literally warn: "Emotions may come up"
- "In pigeon pose, nobody can see you cry" (actual yoga culture saying)
- "The hips store what the mind won't process"
- People report crying, rage, grief, relief — all from a hip stretch
- Teachers cue: "Stay with whatever comes up" / "Let it move through you"

### Downward Dog (Adho Mukha Svanasana)
- Literally a DOG pose. In a cat's world, this is comedy.
- "Press the floor away from you" / "Send your hips up and back"
- It's both a resting pose AND a working pose — the paradox

### Crow Pose (Bakasana)
- The first arm balance most people learn
- Teacher cues: "Lean forward until you have to fly" / "Trust that your arms will hold you"
- "You can't get into crow without committing"

### Eagle Pose (Garudasana)
- Wrapping everything tight, binding yourself up, then BALANCING
- "Sometimes you have to get tangled up to find your center"

### Half Moon (Ardha Chandrasana)
- "Reach in the direction you want to go"
- "Open up even when it feels like you might fall"

### Bridge Pose (Setu Bandha Sarvangasana)
- "Lift your hips. Open your heart."
- "Press your feet down to lift yourself up"

## 3. TEACHER CORRECTIONS / ALIGNMENT CUES

These are the ADJUSTMENTS — what teachers say when they see you doing it wrong. Physical instructions that are accidentally therapy.

### Body Organization
- "Stack your joints"
- "Draw your navel to your spine"
- "Micro-bend, don't lock"
- "Gaze past your fingertips"
- "Hips square to the front"
- "Stack your shoulders over your wrists"
- "Keep your knees tracking over your toes"
- "Align your front heel with your back arch"
- "Lengthen before you deepen"
- "Build from the ground up"

### Tension Release (These are the BEST ones for MaybeCat)
- "Release the tension in your jaw"
- "Drop your shoulders away from your ears"
- "Soften your face"
- "Unclench your fists"
- "Let go of your tongue from the roof of your mouth"
- "Soften the space between your eyebrows"
- "Release the grip in your toes"
- "Stop holding on so tight"
- "Soften the tops of your shoulders"
- "Keep the base of your neck soft"
- "Let your eyes get heavy"
- "Soften any gripping"
- "Relax your hands — they don't need to work right now"

### Core / Center
- "Engage your core"
- "Find your center"
- "Draw everything to your midline"
- "Hollow your belly"
- "Stabilize from the inside out"
- "Your core is your home base"

### Direction / Reach
- "Reach in opposite directions"
- "Energy out through your fingertips"
- "Crown of the head toward the ceiling"
- "Tailbone toward the floor"
- "Lengthen from crown to tailbone"
- "Reach through your crown"
- "Extend through every limb"
- "Take up more space"

### Foundation
- "Root the four corners of your feet into the mat"
- "Press down to lift up"
- "Find your foundation first"
- "Ground before you reach"
- "You can only go as high as your roots go deep"
- "Spread your fingers wide — more contact, more stability"
- "Feel the earth beneath you"

## 4. THE HARD TRUTHS OF YOGA INSTRUCTION

What teachers say that hits different when it's about life.

### On Difficulty
- "This is supposed to be uncomfortable"
- "The pose you avoid is the one you need"
- "Comfort is not where growth happens"
- "If it were easy, everyone would do it"
- "The shaking means it's working"
- "Stay with the discomfort — don't flee from it"
- "You're not supposed to be good at this yet"

### On Showing Up
- "Your body is different today than yesterday"
- "Some days you're stronger. Some days you're not. Both are valid"
- "The hardest part is getting on the mat"
- "You showed up. That's enough"
- "Practice, not perfect"
- "There is no perfect pose. There's YOUR pose"

### On Comparison
- "Stay on your own mat"
- "Comparison is the thief of practice"
- "Your neighbor's body is not your body"
- "Close your eyes if you need to"
- "Your practice is between you and your mat"

### On Surrender
- "You can't force flexibility"
- "Sometimes the bravest thing is to rest"
- "Not every day is a backbend day"
- "Honor where you are, not where you think you should be"
- "The pose doesn't care about your ego"
- "Modify without apology"
- "Props are not cheating"
- "Listen to your body more than you listen to me"

## 5. SAVASANA CUES — PURE CAT ENERGY

The ending relaxation. Teachers guide you into doing NOTHING. This is the most cat thing in all of yoga.

### The Surrender
- "Let go of any effort"
- "Let the floor hold you"
- "Nothing to do, nowhere to go"
- "Surrender the weight of your body"
- "Let your eyes get heavy"
- "Give your weight to the earth"
- "Stop trying"
- "Let gravity do all the work"
- "Surrender to the ground beneath you"
- "Release the need to control anything"
- "There's nothing left to do"

### The Stillness
- "Be still"
- "Let yourself be held"
- "Allow the stillness to find you"
- "Don't fix anything"
- "Nothing needs to change right now"
- "Just be" (sounds foo-foo but said POST-workout it hits different)
- "Nowhere to be but here"
- "Your only job is to lie here"
- "Let the practice settle into your body"

### The Body Scan
- "Soften your jaw"
- "Let your tongue drop from the roof of your mouth"
- "Feel the weight of your eyelids"
- "Let your fingers uncurl"
- "Relax the space behind your eyes"
- "Let your whole body become heavy"
- "Feel yourself sinking into the mat"
- "Release every muscle you can name, and then the ones you can't"
- "Let your bones be heavy"
- "Melt into the floor"
- "Let your skin soften"

### The Coming Back
- "Start to deepen your breath"
- "Begin to wiggle your fingers and toes"
- "Take your time — there's no rush"
- "When you're ready — and only when you're ready"
- "Come back slowly"
- "Carry this feeling with you"

## 6. ALIGNMENT LANGUAGE AS METAPHOR

The technical vocabulary of yoga that doubles as life framework.

### Stack
- Building from the ground up
- "Stack your spine" — vertebra by vertebra
- "Stack your joints" — everything in alignment
- "If the foundation is off, everything above it is off"

### Engage
- "Engage your core" — activate your center of strength
- "Engage your bandhas" — internal energy locks
- "You can be soft AND engaged at the same time"

### Open
- "Open your heart" — literally a chest opener instruction
- "Open your hips" — emotional release territory
- "Open your palms" — receiving, not grasping
- "Open your chest to the ceiling" — vulnerability

### Drishti (Focal Point / Gaze)
- "Find your drishti" — where you look determines your balance
- "Soft gaze" — see without staring
- "Gaze past your fingertips" — look beyond what's right in front of you
- "Fix your eyes on something that isn't moving"
- "Where your gaze goes, your energy follows"

### Bandha (Lock / Seal)
- Internal energy seals — holding energy IN
- Mula bandha (root lock) — your foundation
- Uddiyana bandha (abdominal lock) — your center
- Jalandhara bandha (throat lock) — your expression
- "Seal your energy in" — keep your power for yourself

### Root
- "Root down" — establish your base before doing anything else
- "Root through your standing foot" — trust what's holding you up
- "Root to rise" — you go up by going down first
- "Find your roots before you reach"

### Space
- "Create space" — between your vertebrae, in your joints, in your breath
- "Find the space between effort and ease"
- "Make space for what's coming"
- "Space in the body, space in the mind"
- "Breathe into the spaces"

## 7. YIN YOGA / LONG HOLD CUES

Yin yoga holds poses for 3-5 minutes. The instruction language during holds is PEAK cat energy — sitting with discomfort, observing without reacting.

### Staying In It
- "Find your appropriate edge and wait"
- "Stay with the sensation"
- "Observe without reacting"
- "This isn't something to solve. Just feel it"
- "Sensation is a conversation, not a confrontation"
- "Notice what arises. Let it"
- "You don't have to do anything about it"
- "Sit with it"
- "Be curious about the discomfort, not afraid of it"
- "Resist the urge to fidget"
- "The stillness IS the practice"
- "Allow what's happening to happen"

### Time / Duration
- "We're going to be here for a while"
- "Settle in"
- "You have time"
- "Let time soften you"
- "The longer you stay, the more you feel"
- "Two more minutes" (always feels like ten)

How teachers describe what you SHOULD be feeling. Body language that works as emotional language.

### Sensation Cues
- "Burning in the quads" — it's working
- "Opening in the hips" — letting go of something stored
- "Length in the spine" — growing into yourself
- "Space between the vertebrae" — room to breathe
- "Heat building" — transformation happening
- "Tingling in the hands" — energy moving
- "Heaviness in the legs" — grounding
- "Lightness in the chest" — freedom
- "Expansion through the ribs" — taking up space
- "Softening in the belly" — letting your guard down

### The Paradox Cues (These are GOLD)
- "Effort without strain"
- "Strong but not rigid"
- "Stable but not stuck"
- "Soft but not collapsed"
- "Active rest"
- "Comfortable discomfort"

- "Find ease in the effort"

## 9. THE BIKRAM DIALOGUE — SCRIPTED INSTRUCTION

Bikram (hot yoga) teachers memorize an EXACT script. Same words every class. These hit like mantras:

- "Lock the knee!"
- "Go beyond your flexibility!"
- "Push! Push! Push!"
- "All over equally the same"
- "Keep pushing!"
- "Continuous, constant, never-ending"
- "Don't be afraid"
- "More! More! More!"
- "Beyond your capacity"

(Note: The Bikram dialogue is its own beast — militant, repetitive, almost drill-sergeant. Different energy but some phrases work.)

## 10. TEACHER META-LANGUAGE — HOW TEACHERS TALK ABOUT TEACHING

The vocabulary yoga teachers use when discussing their craft with each other.

### Common Teacher-to-Teacher Language
- "Hold space" — create a safe container for whatever comes up
- "Internal vs. external cues" — feel-based vs. visual instruction
- "Meet them where they are" — adjust to the student's level
- "Offer, don't impose" — suggestions, not commands
- "Invitation language" — "I invite you to..." / "If it's available to you..."
- "Honor the body" — respect physical limitations
- "Listen to the body's wisdom" — the body knows things the mind doesn't
- "Modification is not lesser" — different is not worse
- "Every body is different" — there's no one right way

### Invitation vs. Command
- Teachers debate: "Step your right foot forward" vs. "If you'd like, you might step your right foot forward"
- "Try" vs. "Do"
- "Invite your arms overhead" vs. "Raise your arms"
- The language of OFFERING rather than demanding — the cat energy is in having the confidence of the command version
